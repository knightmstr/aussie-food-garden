# Insect and mite pests: organic control

> **Read first:** use any product exactly as its label or permit says; registration status is only given where a source shows it. Spray late in the day and avoid flowering plants to protect bees ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/animals-and-livestock/bees/management/pesticides2/pesticides-reducing-damage-to-honey-bees), [SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/pesticides-most-harmful-to-bees)). Nothing on this page confirms that a product is registered for home-garden use.

## The short version

- Monitor first. Check leaf undersides with a hand lens weekly (twice weekly in summer) and use yellow or blue sticky traps for thrips and whitefly ([AUSVEG Mega Pests](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf)).
- Broad-spectrum sprays cause secondary outbreaks of mites, scale and whitefly, because they kill the natural enemies ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf), [Business Queensland](https://business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/horticultural/two-spotted-mite)).
- Prevent and exclude where you can. Netting and sanitation have the strongest evidence for fruit fly ([Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/532139/Managing-Queensland-fruit-fly-in-your-home-garden.pdf)).
- Encourage beneficials with flowers and by sparing them. Alyssum, cornflower and buckwheat are backed by Australian trials ([Hort Innovation VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)).
- Use physical controls next: squash, hose off, hand-pick, trap, bag, net.
- Use the softest product last, only on hotspots, and only where the target is named on the label.
- Viruses cannot be cured. Thrips, whitefly and aphids spread them, so remove infected plants ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf)).
- "Organic" does not mean bee-safe. Pyrethrin, for example, can still harm bees ([SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/pesticides-most-harmful-to-bees)).

## Protect bees and beneficials when you spray

- Spray only when necessary. Pick the lowest bee-hazard, shortest-residual product, and spray late afternoon or early evening ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/animals-and-livestock/bees/management/pesticides2/pesticides-reducing-damage-to-honey-bees)).
- Avoid drift onto flowers or hives. Tell neighbouring beekeepers 48 hours ahead. Liquids or granules can be less hazardous than dusts. Mow flowering weeds instead of spraying them ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/animals-and-livestock/bees/management/pesticides2/pesticides-reducing-damage-to-honey-bees)). That page does not rate oils, soaps, pyrethrum, spinosad or kaolin.
- Natural-sounding pyrethrin products are not necessarily safe for bees. Avoid daytime spraying. Residues wane after about a week. Neonicotinoids, organophosphates, synthetic pyrethroids and fipronil are named as harmful to bees ([SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/pesticides-most-harmful-to-bees)). SGA does not cover spinosad, neem, soaps or oils.
- Cucurbits need bees to set fruit, so minimise insecticide during flowering ([NT DAF](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/233628/805.pdf)).
- The spinosad bait label says not to spray plants in flower when bees are active. The "harmful only if ingested" claim comes from the manufacturer and is not independently verified ([PIRSA/SARDI technote](https://pir.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/497543/Naturalure-Bee-Safety-Technote.pdf)).
- Beneficials recover slowly after chemical use, and they need some pest food to stay ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)).
- Hort Innovation's project VG16067 rates pesticide impact on beneficials, but the sources reviewed could not retrieve the ratings ([AUSVEG VG16067](https://ausveg.com.au/knowledge-hub/impact-of-pesticides-on-beneficial-arthropods-of-importance-in-australian-vegetable-production-vg16067/)). The VG16062 factsheet says nothing on pesticide impacts either.

## Plants that bring in beneficials

Australian trials (Hort Innovation VG16062, vegetable crops 2018 to 2020): 1 m flowering strips beside brassicas lifted beneficials. They stayed elevated at least 15 m into the crop, and diamondback moth parasitism more than doubled. Caterpillar suppression was only a trend, because pest numbers were very low ([VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)). Evidence grade: moderate.

| Plant | What the trials found | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Sweet alyssum | Flowers longest. Attracts carabid and rove beetles, Orius and red and blue beetles. Not known to attract brassica pests. | Trials were in brassicas, in southern conditions. |
| Cornflower | Best for boosting parasitoid wasps and some generalist predators. | None given in the sources reviewed. |
| Common buckwheat | Quick, early nectar. | Least effective for parasitoids, flowers briefly in spring, frost tender. |

- Layout used: strips 1 m wide about 30 m apart, for example along sprinkler rows ([VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)).
- Coriander was not tested, and jassids (minor pests) were favoured ([VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)).
- Yellow rocket is a diamondback moth trap crop (larvae cannot complete development on it) but was hard to establish from seed in drought ([VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)). Sorghum as a banker plant has international evidence only.
- A council guide also names feverfew, parsley and yarrow (Asteraceae and Apiaceae) and says hoverflies need shallow flowers. Perennials give permanent homes ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)). No trial data was seen.
- Native insectaries within about 50 m of crops are recommended, but the 2019 article reports no measured results ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/article/creating-a-permanent-habitat-for-beneficials-through-insectaries/)). In an orchard trial in Victoria, buckwheat and chicory/yarrow were marginally best, and mustard raised russet and mite numbers ([Hort Innovation AP00033](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/historic-reports/orchard-habitat-management-to-enhance-ipm-systems-in-pome-fruit-ap00033.pdf)).
- Weak or mixed: marigold and mint did not repel onion or cabbage root fly, and nasturtium is "a pretty aphid nursery" unless pests are removed ([Parker et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/322420712.pdf), [Happy Valley Seeds](https://www.happyvalleyseeds.com.au/blogs/companion-planting/companion-planting-australia)). See [Principles](../principles.md) for the full companion-planting grading.

## Sap-suckers

### Aphids

**Recognise:** malformed new growth, ants on stems, sticky honeydew and sooty mould. Aphids favour yellow flowers, warm moist conditions and soft growth on roses, many vegetables and citrus ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)). Green peach aphid honeydew leads to sooty mould ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf)). Aphids are the worst brassica pest named in the Alice Springs broccoli and cabbage guide ([NT Agnote B37](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/233029/768.pdf)). They can spread cucumber and lettuce mosaic viruses. No specific aphid threshold is given in the sources reviewed.

**The escalation ladder** (council and SGA home-garden sources agree on this order; no efficacy trials were seen):

1. **Squash or hose off.** First option for small colonies ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)).
2. **Manage the ants** (see below), so predators can reach the colony.
3. **Bring in beneficials.** Spotted ladybird (Harmonia) eats aphids. Green lacewing (Mallada signata) is a generalist. Hoverflies need shallow flowers. Brown lacewings and ladybirds also feature in the Hort Innovation trials ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf), [VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)).
4. **Other low-effort options:** sticky traps, a yellow water dish, aluminium foil at the plant base. These are weak or anecdotal, with no trial evidence in the sources reviewed.
5. **Soap or oil, last resort.** The council guide lists horticultural soap or pyrethrum as the last resort. Potassium-salt soaps and white or plant oils are listed for aphids in the biopesticide factsheet ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)). See [Products explained](#products-explained) for cautions.
6. **Winter or pest oil on bark** smothers eggs on fruit trees ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)).

**Cautions on the ladder:**
- Soap and oil only work on contact, and they hit beneficials too. Treat hotspots only.
- Pyrethrum is non-specific, lasts about 24 hours, and can kill bees and beneficials. Some products include synthetic synergists and are not organic-certified ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf), [VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)).
- Avoid spraying flowers while bees are active.

**Ants farming aphids:** ants protect aphids and scale from predators and farm them for honeydew. The council guide suggests glue bands on trunks, controlling ants that shield scale, and a home-made borax-sugar bait. Tansy and pennyroyal are said to repel ants, but that is a companion-planting claim with no evidence given ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)). Keep borax baits away from children and pets [general knowledge].

**Attractant plants:** see [Plants that bring in beneficials](#plants-that-bring-in-beneficials). Alyssum and cornflower strips help predators and parasitoids. Nasturtium can turn into an aphid nursery.

**Sooty mould link:** sooty mould grows on aphid honeydew, so the fix is controlling aphids and ants, not treating the black coating. Fungal diseases are on [diseases and weeds](diseases.md).

**Evidence:** moderate. Two council and society sources agree on the ladder, but the sources reviewed show no efficacy trials for the individual steps. Foil, yellow dish and ant-repelling plants are weak or anecdotal.

**Sources:** [Yarra Ranges Council guide](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf), [AUSVEG Mega Pests](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf), [VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf).

### Whitefly

**Recognise:** yellow sticky traps and a hand-lens check of leaf undersides. Silverleaf whitefly saliva can cause extra plant damage. It peaks late spring to early summer, and occurs year-round in warmer climates ([SGA whitefly](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/whitefly/)). A young cucurbit crop with ten adults per trap per week signals a need to treat ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf)).

**Beneficials and biological control:** ladybirds, lacewings and hoverflies eat nymphs and adults. Parasitoids are Encarsia and Eretmocerus, and Deraeocoris signatus is a predatory bug. Encarsia formosa is sold commercially for greenhouses. SGA suggests marigold and alyssum nearby ([SGA](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/whitefly/), [AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf)).

**Physical controls:** hose or shake plants for 3 or more days running when numbers are small. Low-suction vacuuming works (freeze the bag contents). Yellow sticky traps at about one per 3 m x 3 m also catch beneficials, so SGA does not prefer them ([SGA](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/whitefly/)).

**Organic sprays and products:** sprays must contact the insect and reach the leaf undersides. SGA lists soap, botanical oils, and home garlic or chilli (no rates given). The council guide gives soap (Natrasoap named) every 2 to 3 days for two weeks, with neem and pyrethrum as a last resort and Eco-Oil for scale and whitefly. Garlic or chilli "may deter" whitefly: low evidence.

**Evidence:** moderate. Silverleaf whitefly "readily develops resistance to insecticides" and transmits viruses ([NSW DPI silverleaf](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/agriculture/horticulture/vegetables/diseases-pests-disorders/d-p-d/pests/silverleaf)). That page gives no organic detail. Greenhouse whitefly was not separately sourced.

**Sources:** [SGA whitefly](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/whitefly/), [Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf), [NSW DPI silverleaf](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/agriculture/horticulture/vegetables/diseases-pests-disorders/d-p-d/pests/silverleaf).

### Thrips

**Recognise:** monitor with yellow or blue sticky traps and a hand lens. Thrips spread tomato spotted wilt virus (TSWV), and rogueing infected plants helped ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf)). No thrips threshold is given.

**Beneficials and biological control:** predators are Amblyseius cucumeris, Orius armatus (effective at about 8 per m2 in capsicum trials) and Hypoaspis aculeifer (pupae). Pollen-producing flowers sustain Orius when pest numbers are low. Brown lacewing and ladybirds also eat thrips ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2013/09/MegaPestsManagingSuckingPests.pdf), [VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)).

**Organic sprays and products:** potassium soap and white oil are listed for thrips ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)). Treat hotspots only so predators can build up. Basil and marigold reduced thrips on tomato in some US studies ([UMN Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/planting-and-growing-guides/companion-planting-home-gardens)); this is non-Australian.

**Evidence:** moderate. No thrips-specific organic control source was fetched beyond predators.

**Sources:** as linked above.

### Two-spotted mite

**Recognise:** adults under 0.5 mm with two dark spots under a x10 lens. Yellow stippling near veins on leaf undersides, clear spherical eggs, overwintering females are orange. Hot, dry weather drives outbreaks. Hosts include strawberry, beans, tomato, stonefruit, apples, pears, papaya, roses and weeds ([Business Queensland](https://business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/horticultural/two-spotted-mite)).

**Prevent:** keep vegetation between rows, avoid bare dusty soil, use windbreaks, avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum sprays. Mite outbreaks often follow excess insecticide use, so avoid calendar spraying ([Business Queensland](https://business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/horticultural/two-spotted-mite), [NSW DPI](https://dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/1560596/Two-spotted-mite.pdf)).

**Beneficials and biological control:** Stethorus lady beetles, Amblyseius, Phytoseiulus persimilis, lacewings, predatory thrips. NSW DPI says Neoseiulus californicus, P. persimilis and Typhlodromus occidentalis are commercially available. Predatory mites are uncommon in tropical north Queensland. Commercial action thresholds exist (NSW DPI tree fruit: over 20% of leaves with no predators; Queensland: 50% of leaves), but they are for commercial crops, not gardens.

**Organic sprays and products:** Eco-Oil (tomato, cucumber, capsicum), soaps and white oil are listed for mites ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)). Avoid spraying in hot weather ([Business Queensland](https://business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/horticultural/two-spotted-mite)).

**Evidence:** strong for biological and cultural control; moderate for oil and soap.

**Sources:** [Business Queensland](https://business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/horticultural/two-spotted-mite), [NSW DPI](https://dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/1560596/Two-spotted-mite.pdf), [VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf).

### Scale and mealybug

**Beneficials and biological control:** the mealybug destroyer Cryptolaemus montrouzieri is sold by Bugs For Bugs as adults (packs of 100) or larvae. It targets citrus, obscure and citrophilus mealybug, black, pulvinaria and cottony cushion scale. It works best at 25 to 28 C, and you should wait at least 4 weeks after organophosphates, carbamates or synthetic pyrethroids. Its larvae look like mealybugs ([Bugs For Bugs](https://bugsforbugs.com.au/product/cryptolaemus/)). Other beneficials: Chilocorus circumdatus (armoured scale), Aphytis wasps (red and armoured scale) ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)).

**Prevent:** ants protect these pests, so manage ants (see [Aphids](#aphids)).

**Organic sprays and products:** soap targets mealybug ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)). White oil smothers scale, and Eco-Oil is listed for scale ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)). Home oil rates for scale were not obtained. The oil cautions under [Citrus leafminer](#citrus-leafminer) apply.

**Evidence:** moderate (supplier and council sources).

**Sources:** as linked above.

### True bugs

Covers harlequin bug, Rutherglen bug and fruit-spotting bug.

**Recognise:** harlequin bug hosts include cabbage, tomato, beans, asparagus, figs and apples, and swarms gather on tree trunks, posts and sheds. Rutherglen bug breeds on weeds then moves to crops such as carrots, beans and Solanaceae ([Hort Innovation NY15002](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/ny15002-true-bugs-factsheet.pdf)). Fruit-spotting bug occurs north of about 30 degrees latitude (including the NT), attacks 22+ fruit and nut crops, and losses above 50% have been recorded ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/biosecurity/insect-pests/fruitspotting-bugs)). That page has no control detail.

**Prevent and physical controls:** remove harlequin egg rafts by hand, target nymphs just after hatching, remove weeds, wood piles and overwintering sites, and use very fine mesh screens ([NY15002](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/ny15002-true-bugs-factsheet.pdf)). For Rutherglen bug, control weeds (especially fleabane), plant summer crops early and trench edges to stop nymph movement (CSIRO, grain focus) ([CSIRO](https://research.csiro.au/agroecology/wp-content/uploads/sites/416/2023/11/RGB_Management.pdf)).

**Beneficials:** assassin, damsel and pirate bugs help ([NY15002](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/ny15002-true-bugs-factsheet.pdf)).

**Organic sprays and products:** no organic spray was verified for these bugs. The factsheet's chemical list is synthetic except neem (azadirachtin), which it lists as broad-spectrum. Bagging fruit against fruit-spotting bug is unverified.

**Evidence:** moderate for cultural control.

## Chewers

### Caterpillars

Covers cabbage white butterfly, diamondback moth, Helicoverpa and loopers.

**Prevent and biological control:** the diamondback moth parasitoid Diadegma semiclausum is boosted by cornflower and alyssum strips, and yellow rocket works as a trap crop ([VG16062](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg16062-boosting-beneficials-in-your-vegetable-crop.pdf)). Trichogramma wasps parasitise moth eggs ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)). Helicoverpa NPV is specific to Helicoverpa and Heliothis larvae ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)).

**Physical controls:** hand-picking and net exclusion have no fetched source (general practice, unverified). Cabbage white specifics beyond Bt were not sourced.

**Organic sprays and products, Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis):** works on caterpillars only, and only if they eat it, so cover leaves thoroughly. B. t. kurstaki affects all Lepidoptera, so it also hits harmless butterfly and moth caterpillars. Aizawai is labelled for brassicas ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf)). The DiPel DF label (an agricultural pack, not a home pack) lists Helicoverpa, diamondback moth, cabbage white, loopers and armyworm. It says to spray late afternoon or evening, start at egg hatch or early larvae, repeat every 3 to 5 days under pressure, use within 12 hours of mixing, and avoid rain within 8 hours ([APVMA label](https://elabels.apvma.gov.au/53431ELBL.pdf)). Rates on that label are for commercial packs, so follow your own product's label.

**Evidence:** strong for Bt; moderate for flower strips.

**Sources:** as linked above.

### Cutworm

**Recognise:** larvae grow to 50 mm, curl into a C when handled, feed at night and hide in soil by day. Seedlings are most at risk. Find them by scratching the soil ([Business Queensland](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/field-crop/cutworm)).

**Prevent:** control weeds 3 to 4 weeks before sowing. Wasp parasitoids and fungal diseases suppress cutworm. Business Queensland suggests spot-treating in the evening (field-crop source).

**Organic sprays and products:** the Bt label lists armyworm and loopers but not cutworm (Agrotis), so do not assume Bt works on cutworm ([APVMA label](https://elabels.apvma.gov.au/53431ELBL.pdf)).

**Evidence:** moderate (field-crop source applied to gardens [inference]).

### Cucumber and pumpkin beetles

**Recognise:** yellow or orange beetles 6 to 7 mm. The pumpkin beetle (Aulacophora hilaris) has four black spots. Seedlings are most vulnerable and a few beetles can kill them ([NT DAF](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/233628/805.pdf)).

**Prevent:** check twice weekly and remove crop residues after harvest. Netting seedlings is unsourced.

**Organic sprays and products:** NT DAF suggests potassium soaps as a soft option but gives no products or rates, and efficacy on beetles is not demonstrated. Minimise insecticide during flowering because cucurbits need bees.

**Evidence:** moderate for monitoring; weak for soap on beetles.

### Grasshoppers

**Recognise and prevent:** most are solitary, while locusts aggregate. Dense grassy pasture limits egg-laying, so control weeds and grass. Act at the first sign of nymph aggregation ([Business Queensland](https://business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/insects/field-crop/grasshoppers)).

**Beneficials:** Scelio wasps parasitise up to 30% of eggs. Birds and guinea fowl help.

**Organic sprays and products:** a commercial Metarhizium anisopliae biopesticide exists, but the page gives no application detail. Garden-scale netting, spinosad or neem efficacy was not found.

**Evidence:** weak for gardens. The source is for pasture and field crops.

### Vegetable leafminer

**Recognise:** leaf mines alone do not show a need to act. Check for live larvae or parasitism with a lens, and monitor adults with yellow sticky traps at canopy height ([AUSVEG leafminer plan](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2020/12/Management-Plan-Exotic-leafminers.pdf)).

**Beneficials and biological control:** parasitoid wasps are the most effective control (field mortality about 80%). Australia has 50+ native parasitoids (Hemiptarsenus varicornis, Diglyphus isaea, Opius and others). None is mass-reared in Australia for leafminer. Weed leafminers on sowthistle and plantain may host the wasps.

**Organic sprays and products:** broad-spectrum insecticides kill the wasps but not the larvae inside leaves, which causes outbreaks. Avoid unnecessary sprays and tolerate cosmetic mining on leafy crops [inference, from the parasitoid evidence in a commercial-crop plan].

**Legal:** the exotic L. sativae is under quarantine at Seisia, Qld, and American serpentine leafminer restricts movement of leafy vegetables, herbs and soil out of parts of the Kimberley ([DPIRD WA](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/plant-biosecurity/movement-restrictions-plants-within-western-australia)). Report rather than treat in those areas.

**Evidence:** moderate (commercial-crop source).

## Fruit pests

### Queensland fruit fly and Medfly

**Recognise and where:** Qfly hosts include peach, orange, apple, pear, tomato and capsicum ([SGA Qfly](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/queensland-fruit-fly/)). Medfly infests 200+ plant species including fruiting vegetables, is established in parts of WA, and soft stone fruit are the most attractive hosts there ([Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/biosecurity/pest-insects-and-mites/priority-pest-insects-and-mites/mediterranean-fruit-fly), [DPIRD WA](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/sites/gateway/files/Medfly%20Backyard%20management.pdf)). Winter shelter for Qfly includes large-leaved evergreens such as avocado, citrus and loquat ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/dpi/biosecurity/invasive-plants-and-animals/insect-pests/queensland-fruit-fly)). Davidson's plum (D. jerseyana) is susceptible; control is by traps and bagging fallen fruit in the sun for 3 to 7 days ([SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/davidsons-plum)).

**Prevent (strongest evidence):**
- Mesh: fine UV-stable mesh over a frame after pollination, sealed at the trunk, with fruit kept off the mesh because Qfly can sting through. The sources reviewed give no mesh size ([Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/532139/Managing-Queensland-fruit-fly-in-your-home-garden.pdf)). DPIRD WA suggests mosquito netting, shade cloth or nylon flyscreen over a frame, only while fruit ripens ([DPIRD WA](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/medfly/control-options-backyards)). Also bag individual fruit or branches in gauze, muslin or mosquito netting.
- Sanitation: pick ripe fruit before it falls and collect fallen fruit. Kill maggots by freezing, microwaving, boiling or solarising (sealed bag in sun for at least 14 days in Victoria; 3 to 7 days in Queensland and WA sources). Do not compost infested fruit. DPIRD WA also lists burying at least 1 m deep. Bananas and avocados can be picked mature-green ([SGA Qfly](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/queensland-fruit-fly/)). In Alice Springs, infested fruit is bagged in the sun for two weeks or soaked ([Alice Springs News](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2021/01/18/january-gardening-in-facts-and-figures/)). The sources differ on times, so follow your state agency.
- In winter, bait refuge trees and keep baiting through autumn. Empty winter traps do not mean the flies are gone ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/dpi/biosecurity/invasive-plants-and-animals/insect-pests/queensland-fruit-fly)). Chickens scratching for pupae are mentioned by SGA.

**Traps:** hang traps about 1.5 m high in shade, set up 6 to 8 weeks before ripening (Victoria). Types are para-pheromone (males), protein (both sexes) and fruit-scent. Traps alone are unlikely to manage Qfly and can catch beneficials ([Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/532139/Managing-Queensland-fruit-fly-in-your-home-garden.pdf)). DPIRD WA's Medfly page says at least two homemade traps per tree, 1.5 to 2 m high, at least four 6 to 8 mm holes, and replace the lure weekly ([DPIRD WA](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/medfly/control-options-backyards)). It gives four lure recipes, for example 80 g sugar, 1.5 g dry brewer's yeast and 920 mL water. Agriculture Victoria gives a bottle trap of 3 cups juice and 3 tbsp cloudy ammonia, changed weekly and kept from pets and children. Ceratrap is named as the only registered attract-and-kill device in WA ([DPIRD WA](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/sites/gateway/files/Medfly%20Backyard%20management.pdf)); this is unverified for other states.

**Organic sprays and products, spinosad bait:** Naturalure is the organic-certified option for fruiting vegetables. DPIRD WA says to start when fruit is about one-third final size, apply coarse droplets of at least 2 mm as a spot spray on foliage (not on fruit), and reapply weekly and after more than 5 mm of rain; its WA page gives 60 to 100 mL per tree. Avoid flowering plants when bees are active ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf), [PIRSA technote](https://pir.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/497543/Naturalure-Bee-Safety-Technote.pdf)). Maldison is also named but is not organic. Follow the product label or permit.

**Rules:** Medfly is a declared pest in WA with mandatory control in named areas, and Agriculture Victoria describes Victoria as free of Medfly. Do not carry fruit or fruiting vegetables from SA or WA into Victoria ([Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/biosecurity/pest-insects-and-mites/priority-pest-insects-and-mites/mediterranean-fruit-fly)). See the state pages and [biosecurity](../biosecurity-and-weeds.md).

**Evidence:** strong for exclusion and sanitation; moderate for bait; moderate for lure recipes as monitors and weak for suppression; weak for traps alone. DPIRD's Medfly page is flagged as possibly out of date. The sources reviewed do not separately source Medfly baiting for the eastern states, and mesh size is not given.

### Codling moth

**Recognise and biology:** breeding starts above about 15 C average. Eggs hatch in about 10 days, with about 3 generations per season ([PIRSA](https://pir.sa.gov.au/crops-and-plants/weeds-and-plant-pests/plant-pests/common-seasonal-pests/codling_moth)).

**Physical controls:** corrugated cardboard bands on trunk and limbs, checked every 3 weeks then burned. Paling traps (two 30 cm palings with a 5 mm gap) emptied every 3 weeks. Bait bottle traps (molasses, fermented apple juice or diluted port with an oil layer), one per third tree, for monitoring. Sticky bands, clearing loose bark, and removing fallen fruit (burn it or feed to chickens). Bagging was not covered ([PIRSA](https://pir.sa.gov.au/crops-and-plants/weeds-and-plant-pests/plant-pests/common-seasonal-pests/codling_moth)).

**Beneficials:** Trichogramma carverae parasitises codling moth eggs ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)).

**Organic sprays and products:** Eco Oils smother eggs but need reapplying after rain. Pyrethrum kills adults but also harms beneficials. A council guide says kaolin is an effective barrier; see [Products explained](#products-explained) for how thin that evidence is.

**Evidence:** moderate (single state agency source for bands and traps).

### Citrus leafminer

**Recognise:** a tiny silvery moth whose larvae make silvery winding mines in new leaves that then curl. Mature trees (over 4 years old) tolerate it, while young and potted trees are hit hardest. It peaks late summer to autumn, and 2 or more mines per leaf can slow young trees ([SGA](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/ggp-garden-pest-citrus-leaf-miner/), [NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/137634/citrus-leaf-miner.pdf)).

**Prevent:** fertilise in late winter, limit late-summer and autumn fertiliser and water to avoid flushes, prune water sprouts and infested late flush, and prune at the right time (mid to late spring in frost-prone areas).

**Beneficials:** parasitic wasps (Ageniaspis citricola, Cirrospilus, Pnigalio, native Semiolacher petiolatus) and lacewings.

**Organic sprays and products:** oil at 250 to 500 mL per 100 L of water. Start when the flush is under 10 mm and stop when leaves harden or exceed 40 mm. Repeat every 5 to 7 days when warm, 10 to 14 days when cool, and cover both leaf surfaces. Oil deters egg-laying but does not kill larvae in mines. Avoid use above 35 C, on stressed or dry trees, and within a month of sulphur. Eco-oil is a lower-impact alternative, neem is relatively safe for beneficials, and spinosad or spinetoram are harmful to beneficials. Follow the product label or permit.

**Evidence:** moderate to strong (two government and society sources agree).

## Soil and ground pests

### Nematodes

**Recognise:** root-knot nematode shows as root galls 1 to 10 mm, patchy stunting and wilting. It has a wide host range (tomato, capsicum, carrot, cucurbits, lettuce, beans, many fruit) ([Business Queensland](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/diseases/horticultural/root-knot-nematode)). Dig a plant and look for galls before choosing covers.

**Prevent:** rotate to a resistant crop or weed-free bare fallow. Use resistant varieties where they exist (tomato, capsicum, potato). Compost and organic matter encourage nematode predators. Infested soil and plants spread the pest, so do not share soil, tubers or roots ([Business Queensland](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/diseases/horticultural/root-knot-nematode)). Bare fallow raises erosion risk ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/knowledge-hub/cover-crops-to-reduce-root-knot-nematode-populations/)).

**Choose covers by nematode status:** in Queensland glasshouse screening, sunn hemp resisted M. enterolobii, M. incognita and M. javanica, and forage sorghum 'Jumbo' and oat 'Swan' were highly resistant to all three. Most soybean varieties and many biofumigants were susceptible, and a mixture works only if every component is resistant ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/knowledge-hub/cover-crops-to-reduce-root-knot-nematode-populations/)). In wet-tropics cane trials no cover reduced pathogenic nematodes ([Soil CRC](https://soilcrc.com.au/?p=6583)). Biofumigation is unverified; one Queensland trial was inconsistent ([Duff and Firrell 2021](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/8750/1/J.-Duff-and-M.-Firrell-2021-Biofumigation--A-Cover-Crop-Option-12-Months-of-the-Year-to-Manage-Three-Soilborne-Pathogens-Ailing-the-Australian-Vegetable-Industry.pdf).

**Top End:** guava root-knot nematode is concentrated in Darwin's residential suburbs. It infects guava, sunn hemp, Cavalcade and many weeds. Practise "come clean, go clean", keep fallows weed-free, use non-host covers and good compost, and contact NT Plant Pathology (plant.pathology@nt.gov.au) ([NT DAF](https://daf.nt.gov.au/news/2026/root-knot-nematodes-in-the-nt)).

**Marigolds (Tagetes):** the best evidence is rotation, not interplanting. In California field trials, T. patula 'Single Gold' grown as a full-area prior crop reduced root-knot nematode on following tomato. Tomato yield was about 50% higher than after fallow, and following melon yield rose 95%. At one site it matched methyl iodide fumigation and at the other fumigation was better. The trial is cultivar-specific and non-Australian ([PubMed 30818673](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30818673/)). Scattered plants are unlikely to work; plant densely and dig in over a season ([Happy Valley Seeds](https://www.happyvalleyseeds.com.au/blogs/companion-planting/companion-planting-australia)). Alice Springs advice pairs marigolds with heavy compost and mulch ([Alice Springs News](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2021/01/18/january-gardening-in-facts-and-figures/)); that is a local gardening source. Tagetes species available in Australia and their effect on local Meloidogyne species were not sourced.

**Evidence:** rotation, fallow and resistant varieties moderate to strong; compost moderate; marigold moderate (non-Australian, one trial read); biofumigation unverified.

### Snails and slugs

**Prevent:** clear weeds first. Mulch and minimum-till can favour these pests ([DPIRD WA](https://agric.wa.gov.au/pest-animals/snail-and-slug-control?nopaging=1)).

**Physical controls:** traps (buried beer or yeast containers, overturned pots, boards) and barriers. Dry sawdust or ash only work while dry. Copper bands work on trunks if you prune skirts. Ducks are considered the best predator (DPIRD WA). One author (ABC Organic Gardener, 2012) found beer traps, coffee grounds and gravel ineffective, so the anecdotes conflict ([Organic Gardener](https://www.organicgardener.com.au/?p=12392)).

**Organic sprays and products:** iron EDTA baits lower poisoning risk compared with metaldehyde. Bait in autumn (late March to April, in WA) or after the first rains. Iron EDTA pellets are effective but not certified organic (approved as an input). Place bait safely away from children and dogs. Follow the product label.

**Evidence:** iron baits moderate; hand collection and traps weak to moderate.

### Termites

Top End focus. Of the nearly 140 northern termite species most are beneficial, but Giant Northern Termite (Mastotermes darwiniensis) can severely damage or kill trees, and is especially aggressive above the Tropic of Capricorn ([NT DPIR](https://dpir.nt.gov.au/primary-industry/primary-industry-publications/regional-newsletters/tp/top-paddock-december-2016/good-termites-and-bad-termites), [CSIRO](https://research.csiro.au/infratech/wp-content/uploads/sites/38/2024/12/3013_ManagingTermites_WCAG.pdf)).

**Prevent (structures, CSIRO):** keep mulch and soil below the slab and away from weep holes. A concrete strip at least 50 mm below the slab and 300 mm wide separates beds and aids inspection. No plants against external walls. Avoid sprinklers wetting walls and stumps. Keep posts and pergola timbers short of soil on metal stirrups. Stack no firewood against or under the house. Use treated or naturally resistant timber, masonry or concrete. Inspect at least annually.

**Inference:** garden mulch is food and moisture for termites, so use inorganic mulch such as gravel nearest the building [inference; CSIRO does not state this for the Top End].

**Organic sprays and products:** the NT booklet covers only Giant Northern Termite aggregation-drum treatment, which uses fipronil (synthetic), so it is not organic ([NT DPIR booklet](https://dpir.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/227967/termites_booklet.pdf)). No organic treatment (nematodes, neem) has a source.

**Evidence:** moderate for physical prevention; none for organic control. Commercial pest-controller pages were not used.

### Banana weevil borer

**Prevent:** use tissue-culture plants or inspect suckers and reject infested ones. Hot water at 54 C for 20 minutes likely kills eggs and grubs. Remove old corms and fallow 3 months before replanting, clear weeds and trash, and cut fallen stems short to dry. Pheromone (sordidin) traps exist, and 4 or more weevils per pseudostem disc signals treatment ([Pacific Pests factsheet 109](https://apps.lucidcentral.org/ppp_v9/pdf/web_full/banana_weevil_109.pdf)). The weevil is recorded in Australia, and banana weevil borer can also spread Panama TR4 ([Business Queensland, Panama TR4](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/crops/fruit-veg/bananas/panama-disease-tr4/identify)).

**Organic sprays and products:** the factsheet names synthetic products only. Check the APVMA.

**Evidence:** moderate. The source is a Pacific factsheet, not Australian-authored. Home patches: clean suckers and tidy harvested stems [inference]. Banana weevil borer restricts movement of banana plants and soil in parts of Wyndham-East Kimberley ([DPIRD WA](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/plant-biosecurity/movement-restrictions-plants-within-western-australia)).

### Earwigs and ants

**Earwigs:** they attack tips, flowers, fruit and seedling roots and thrive under organic mulch. Traps are soy sauce under a thin oil film in a buried container, a beer jar on its side, or crumpled newspaper in a pot destroyed daily ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)). Evidence: weak (anecdotal traps).

**Ants:** ants are a pest mostly because they protect aphids, scale and mealybug. See [Aphids](#aphids) for glue bands, borax-sugar bait and the unproven repellent plants. Evidence: weak to moderate.

## Birds, possums and flying foxes

Exclusion is the non-lethal option. Queensland Government netting spec ([Queensland Government](https://qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/bats/flying-foxes/managing-impacts-of-flying-foxes/netting-fruit-trees)):

- Knitted mesh with openings smaller than 5 x 5 mm when stretched, with strands at least 500 microns. White is preferred. Avoid thin monofilament and shade cloth.
- Build a frame so the net is held off the tree. Pull it taut (bounce test) and peg it to the ground. Never leave it sagging.
- Check at least daily. If an animal is entangled, cover it with a towel and call RSPCA Queensland. Do not handle flying foxes unless vaccinated. Consider removing nets in drought.
- Alternatives: a sheet-metal trunk collar against possums (fails if they can jump from fences or other trees), pruning overhanging branches, and paper bags on reachable fruit.
- Wildlife-unsafe netting is being phased out or banned in some states. The note comes from search snippets only, so check current state law, for example [Victoria](https://www.wildlife.vic.gov.au/managing-wildlife/wildlife-and-fruit-trees).
- A garlic or chilli foliage spray is listed by a council as a possum deterrent only (low evidence) ([Yarra Ranges Council](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf)).
- A commercial exclusion-net page gives no mesh dimension ([Daleys Fruit](https://www.daleysfruit.com.au/exclusion-netting-small.htm)). Insect netting mesh sizes for whitefly, thrips and aphids were not found. A Davidson's plum guide also advises netting against birds, possums and flying foxes ([SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/davidsons-plum)).

**Evidence:** strong for exclusion netting with the spec above.

## Products explained

These notes come from industry factsheets, council guides and retail pages, and not from efficacy trials. Registration status is unverified for every home-garden pack unless stated. Check the APVMA PubCRIS and the product label or permit ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf) tells readers to do this).

| Product | What the sources reviewed say it is for | Evidence | Registration |
|---|---|---|---|
| White (horticultural) oil | Aphids, mites, thrips, some bugs, scale, citrus leafminer egg-laying | Moderate | Vegetable use only under minor use permits (2018 factsheet); home pack unverified |
| Eco-Oil (plant oil) | Mites, aphids, whitefly (tomato, cucumber, capsicum); scale | Moderate | Named in a 2018 factsheet; current status unverified |
| Potassium soap | Aphids, thrips, mealybug, mites, whitefly | Moderate | Hitman, Natrasoap and BugGuard named; no withholding period when used as directed (per 2018 factsheet); current status unverified |
| Neem | Whitefly last resort; relatively safe for beneficials (citrus leafminer source) | Weak as a home spray | No registered neem product listed in 2018 factsheet; home use unverified |
| Pyrethrum | Aphids, whitefly, pear and cherry slug (last resort) | Moderate for effect; harms bees | Unverified |
| Bt | Caterpillars only | Strong | Agricultural DiPel DF label read; home pack not read |
| Spinosad bait | Qfly and Medfly | Moderate | Naturalure named; certified by Australian Organic and OMRI (manufacturer claim); status unverified |
| Kaolin | Barrier against codling moth and others (claimed) | Weak | Unverified |
| Diatomaceous earth | Dry-dust insecticide (claimed) | Weak | Unverified |
| Garlic, chilli, soap home sprays | Whitefly "may deter" | Weak (low evidence) | Not applicable (home recipe); unverified |

- **White oil:** vegetable use is by minor use permit only, so check the label for the home pack. Oil sprays risk plant damage (phytotoxicity) above 35 C or on stressed trees, and should not be used within a month of sulphur (citrus leafminer sources). Oil must contact the pest.
- **Eco-Oil:** the 2018 factsheet lists it for tomato, cucumber and capsicum against two-spotted mite, aphids and greenhouse whitefly. Treat that as a commercial-crop listing.
- **Potassium soap:** listed for aphids, thrips, mealybug, mites and whitefly. The Yarra Ranges guide gives a last-resort every 2 to 3 days for two weeks for whitefly. Contact-only, so it needs good coverage.
- **Neem:** acts as an anti-feedant and life-cycle disruptor, and is a last resort for whitefly (council). It is listed as a plant-extract source only. Harlequin bug factsheet lists azadirachtin as broad-spectrum.
- **Pyrethrum:** non-specific, persistence about 24 hours. Some products include synthetic synergists so are not organic-certified. It can kill bees, beneficials and aquatic life, and SGA says residues wane after about a week ([VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf), [SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/pesticides-most-harmful-to-bees)).
- **Bt:** ingestion only, low impact on beneficials, and the DiPel label says safe to beneficial arthropods. Details under [Caterpillars](#caterpillars).
- **Spinosad bait:** applied as bait, reapplied weekly because sunlight breaks it down. Spinosad and spinetoram sprays are harmful to beneficials (citrus leafminer source). Bee-safety claims are manufacturer claims ([PIRSA technote](https://pir.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/497543/Naturalure-Bee-Safety-Technote.pdf)).
- **Kaolin:** only one source, a council guide naming a US brand (Surround WP), which claims a white clay film that acts as a barrier and reflects sunlight, effective against citrus gall wasp, codling moth, oriental fruit moth, leafhoppers, powdery mildew, stink bugs and thrips. Spray leaves and fruit and avoid flowers. The sources reviewed do not verify it, so its evidence is weak.
- **Diatomaceous earth:** the only source is a vendor article with no studies cited and unsupported claims, so efficacy is unverified. It says pool-grade (calcined) DE is unsuitable, uncalcined DE is needed, it is non-selective and can harm beneficials and pollinators, keep it off open flowers, and dry dust works best ([Dr Greenthumbs, retailer](https://www.drgreenthumbs.com.au/blogs/living-soils-organic-gardening/diatomaceous-earth-australia)).
- **Garlic and chilli:** SGA says home sprays "may deter" whitefly with no rates. The council guide lists garlic or chilli only as a possum-deterrent foliage spray and garlic and chives under citrus as companion planting. No efficacy source was found. Soap in home sprays falls under the soap entry; no home recipe is sourced. Vinegar has no source, so do not use it for pests.
- **Others in the factsheet:** Beauveria bassiana (Broadband OD) is for protected cropping. Copper is phytotoxic at low pH, and sulphur should not be used in hot weather. No essential oil is registered for vegetables.
