# Small spaces, pots and renting

For renters, balconies, courtyards and small backyards. It works with the beginner pages for [Darwin](darwin.md) and [Sydney](sydney.md). The crops and months are on those pages. This page covers the pots, soil, water and rules.

> **How reliable is this page?** The pot advice comes from three councils and agencies: the City of Sydney, NSW Environment, and Salisbury Council in Adelaide ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)). None of them is a Darwin source, and the NT Government pages read for this guide say nothing about pots. Adelaide and Sydney are cooler than Darwin, so treat the figures as a starting point there. Each fact links to its source. Anything not sourced is tagged `[inference]`.

## If you rent or live in a unit

- **Check the rules first.** Apartment residents should check body corporate rules before putting plants on a balcony ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). A lease may also limit what you can do `[general knowledge]`.
- **Use pots, not beds.** Containers are flexible. You can move them and reuse the soil you have improved ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- **Use saucers on balconies** to avoid water running off ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). In general, though, do not leave pots standing in water in a saucer, except briefly in summer while the mix is wetting ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- **Wind and weight:** balconies are windy, so use heavy pots or secure them so they do not blow over ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). If you plan to move pots, choose lightweight pots and good potting mix, because heavy pots are hard to shift ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)).
- **Go up, not out.** Hanging pots, tiered stands, window boxes and sunny walls make more growing space ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). Use a trellis or reinforced mesh for climbers ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)). Tomatoes, peas and beans can be grown in hanging baskets ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)).

## Sun

- Most edibles need about 4 to 6 hours of direct sun a day ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). Salisbury Council says to aim for at least half a day of sun ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- Put pots near a door, window or kitchen so you remember to water and pick ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- Leafy greens, chives, herbs, broccoli, kale and bok choy prefer shade, especially in summer ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)). Protect plants in heat waves with shade cloth or by moving them into shade ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- In Sydney, a north-facing balcony gets the most winter sun, east gets gentle morning sun, and west gets harsher afternoon sun ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)).

## Choose the pot

| Pot depth | Crops it suits | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Shallow, 15 to 20 cm | Spring onion, chives, strawberries, bush beans, short carrots, open lettuce, leafy greens, and herbs such as sage, thyme and oregano | [Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf) |
| Medium, 20 to 30 cm | Brassicas, beetroot, silverbeet, peas and beans, cherry tomatoes, capsicum, chillies, eggplant, garlic, leeks, parsley and rosemary | [Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf) |
| Deep, 30 to 50 cm or more | Larger tomatoes, corn, trellised peas and beans, pumpkins, melons, cucumber, swedes, turnips and potatoes | [Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf) |

- The City of Sydney gives about 20 to 30 cm of soil depth for vegetables and herbs, and about 50 cm for dwarf fruit trees ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- Fewer large pots beat many small ones, which dry out quickly ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- Citrus, dwarf fruit trees, herbs, lettuce, spinach and Asian greens can grow in pots. Some plants do not grow well in pots, so check when you buy ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)).
- Plastic pots hold more moisture than terracotta or concrete ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)). Salisbury says to avoid ceramic pots for annual vegetables, because large ones are hard to empty and heat up quickly ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- **Cheap options:** polystyrene fruit boxes from a greengrocer, with drainage holes added, suit herbs and small vegetables ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). Wash reused plastic pots first ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- **Do not use** treated wood or car tyres as containers ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- **Mint** goes in its own pot because it spreads fast ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).

## Drainage and potting mix

- Pots need 5 to 10 holes in the bottom. Cover the holes with double newspaper or shade cloth so the mix stays in. Ceramic pots often have only one hole, which is usually not enough ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- Buy potting mix that carries the Australian Standard mark. Salisbury advises against non-standard mixes, which can be unpredictable and acidic ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- A regular mix with slow-release fertiliser added at planting is the best value ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)). Shallow pots need a coarser mix, and unglazed pots need a less open mix because they dry out faster ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).

## Watering pots

- Water until it runs out the bottom, and water evenly across the surface ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- Check by lifting the pot and feeling the top 1 to 2 cm of mix ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- If water runs off or pools, the mix is dry. Water slowly or repeat ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)). If the mix is very dry, soak the whole pot in a bucket until bubbles stop ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- Wind dries pots fast, so water often on a balcony ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). If plants wilt, water at once and shade them ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- In summer, water in the morning to reduce fungal problems ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- Summer is the hard part. Salisbury advises not buying more seedlings than you can keep watered ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- Mulch (for example sugar cane) keeps roots cool and means less watering ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). Do not spread mulch so thick that it smothers small plants ([nt.gov.au, tips for new gardens](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/tips-for-new-garden)).
- A tap timer or simple drip system helps when you are away ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).

## Feeding

- Salisbury suggests a thin layer (about 1 cm) of compost on top of the mix, then worm castings, and feeding leafy vegetables differently from fruiting crops (tomato, capsicum, chilli, eggplant, pumpkin, cucumber, zucchini). It gives specific rates, so read them in its guide before measuring ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).
- Over-feeding attracts sap-sucking pests such as aphids and whitefly ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)). The [insect and mite pests page](../pests/insects.md) has organic fixes.
- In big tubs, flush the mix once a year with about twice the pot's volume of water to clear salt build-up ([Salisbury Council, growing in pots and containers](https://www.salisbury.sa.gov.au/assets/files/assets/public/general_documents/live/healthy_living/living_well_in_the_living_city/pack_e1_growing_in_pots_and_containers_final.pdf)).

## Small backyard: the same rules, plus these

- **Raised beds** 10 to 15 cm high drain better. They are part of the Darwin and Katherine sowing sheet advice ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).
- **Add organic matter.** New gardens often have little topsoil ([nt.gov.au, tips for new gardens](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/tips-for-new-garden)).
- **Group plants with similar water needs** and do not overcrowd. Check how big each plant will get ([nt.gov.au, tips for new gardens](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/tips-for-new-garden)).
- **Rotate where plants go each year**, for example broccoli, to reduce clubroot ([Royal Botanic Gardens Sydney, best vegetables for beginners](https://botanicgardens.org.au/discover-and-learn/gardening-home/gardening-tips/best-vegetables-grow-beginners)). The [rotation page](../principles.md) explains which crops follow which.

## Gaps

- No Darwin source on pots. In the heat and humidity of the Top End, pots probably dry out faster in the dry season and can waterlog in the wet `[inference]`. Use drainage holes, raise pots, and shelter them from wind.
- Salisbury's feeding rates and pot sizes come from one council in Adelaide and are not tested in Darwin or Sydney.
- Some pots may need different treatment on a hot Darwin balcony. A local community garden or nursery will know.
