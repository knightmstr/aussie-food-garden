# Start here: Sydney

A short beginner page for people growing food in Sydney, in a backyard, courtyard or balcony. It sits on top of the longer [NSW and ACT page](../states/nsw-act.md), which has the full Sydney sowing table. For pots, balconies and renting, read [small spaces and pots](small-spaces.md) as well.

> **How reliable is this page?** The monthly sowing windows come from one council's seed library list ([City of Ryde seed library guide, June 2024](https://www.ryde.nsw.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/4/library/2024-06-seasonal-planting-guide.pdf)), so they cover only the varieties it stocks and describe a mild temperate northern-Sydney area. Beginner picks and timing notes come from the Royal Botanic Gardens Sydney and the City of Sydney. None of these sources gives frost dates, so suburbs that get frost or are very hot may differ. Each fact links to its source. Anything not sourced is tagged `[inference]`.

## Work out your sun first

- Most edibles need about 4 to 6 hours of direct sun a day to produce well ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).
- In Sydney, north-facing windows and balconies get the most sun in winter. East-facing spots get gentle morning sun and west-facing spots get harsher afternoon sun ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)).
- Balconies can be windy and sunny, so use hardier plants in exposed spots ([NSW Environment, small spaces](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/about-us/get-involved/sydney-nature/gardens/small-spaces)).

Do not copy the Ryde dates for a very frosty or very hot spot. The council list gives no frost advice.

## Common problems

- The NSW page has a section on pests and biosecurity, including fruit fly. See the *Pests and biosecurity here* section of the [NSW and ACT page](../states/nsw-act.md), and [biosecurity and weeds](../biosecurity-and-weeds.md).
- For organic fixes (aphids, caterpillars, whitefly and more), use the [insect and mite pests page](../pests/insects.md). For mildew and leaf problems, use the [diseases page](../pests/diseases.md).
- In summer, water in the morning to reduce fungal problems ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)). Water at the soil so it reaches the roots ([City of Sydney, tips to start an edible garden](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/tips-to-start-an-edible-garden)).

## Gaps

- No frost dates, and no suburb-by-suburb timing. Sydney's coast, west, north and the Blue Mountains edge differ. Check the Bureau of Meteorology for your suburb.
- The Ryde list leaves out many beginner crops. Brassicas (cabbage, cauliflower), peas, garlic, potato, sweet potato and sweet corn have no Sydney months in this guide, apart from the Royal Botanic Gardens Sydney timing for peas and broccoli.
- The January 2024 Ryde edition and other Sydney council calendars were not read.
- This page is for Sydney. Canberra, the Blue Mountains and the north coast need different timing. See the [NSW and ACT page](../states/nsw-act.md).
