# Start here: Darwin and the Top End

A short beginner page for people growing food in Darwin, in a backyard or in pots. It sits on top of the longer [Northern Territory page](../states/nt.md), which has the full sowing table, companions, rotation and the weaker sources. For pots, balconies and renting, read [small spaces and pots](small-spaces.md) as well.

> **How reliable is this page?** The month windows come from the NT Government's Darwin and Katherine sowing sheet ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)), which is government but old (text dated 2005, footer 2008). Other advice comes from NT Government home-garden pages and a Charles Darwin University article. No Darwin-specific pot-growing advice was found, so pot advice is on the small-spaces page with its source (an Adelaide council) and a climate caution. Each fact links to its source. Anything not sourced is tagged `[inference]`.

## The one thing to know: Darwin has two seasons, not four

The Top End wet season runs about November to April and the dry season about May to October ([NT DAF plant industries](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/373645/opportunities-for-plant-industries-in-the-NT.pdf)). On the NT sowing sheet, almost every vegetable is shaded **April to July or August**, which is the start of the dry. Between September and March the sheet shades only a handful of crops ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).

So a beginner's year looks like this:

| Time | What to do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Wet season (about November to March)** | Prepare beds and soil. Sow only the short list of crops the sheet shades for these months (basil, lemon grass, snake beans, sweet potato, rosella from October, and beetroot and shallots in March). Raise any small seed in containers, because from December to February heavy rain washes seed away | [NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf) |
| **Start of the dry (April)** | The main sowing window opens. Most of the sheet's 48 crops are shaded in April, May and June | [NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf) |
| **Dry season (May to August)** | Sow the widest range. Tomato, for example, is shaded May to July on the sheet | [NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf) |
| **Late dry (September to October)** | Few crops are shaded: basil, lemon grass, okra (September), snake beans, sweet potato, and rosella from October | [NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf) |

## Getting ready (works in the wet season)

These steps are from the sheet's own tips and NT Government pages:

1. **Pick a spot with full sun**, no waterlogging in the wet, and wind shelter ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).
2. **Use raised beds at least 10 to 15 cm high** so water drains ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).
3. **Add organic matter.** New gardens often have little topsoil, and organic matter breaks down quickly in the tropics, so top it up regularly ([nt.gov.au, tips for new gardens](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/tips-for-new-garden)).
4. **Mulch straight after planting** to cool the soil, hold moisture and suppress weeds. Do not spread it so thick that it smothers small plants ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf); [nt.gov.au, tips for new gardens](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/tips-for-new-garden)).
5. **Lime and fertiliser:** virgin soils need lime worked in at least six weeks before planting. Work basic fertiliser in two to three weeks before planting ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).
6. **Fertilise and mulch before heavy rain** to help protect soil from erosion. Mulch breaks down faster in the wet ([CDU, wet-season advice, Dec 2014](https://www.cdu.edu.au/news/news-archive/expert-offers-advice-gardeners-wet-season)).
7. **Do not overwater.** The NT page says overwatering can wash away topsoil and fertiliser and waterlog plants. It suggests watering less often but for longer ([nt.gov.au, tips for new gardens](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/tips-for-new-garden)).
8. **Plant out in the late afternoon or on a cool, cloudy day** ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).

## Common problems

- Pests that attack the crops above are listed on each crop's NT page. For example snake beans can be hit by aphids, cutworms, bean flies and mites, and root-knot nematodes ([nt.gov.au, snake beans](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/growing-vegetables-at-home/snake-beans)).
- For organic fixes (aphids, caterpillars, fruit fly and more), use the [insect and mite pests page](../pests/insects.md). For leaf spots, mildew and soil problems, use the [diseases page](../pests/diseases.md).
- The sheet says to dig in or remove crop residue and diseased plants promptly, because pests and diseases build up in leftover residue ([NT DAF IS4](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/228090/is4_vegetable_calendar_darwin_katherine_08.pdf)).

## Before you bring plants or soil in

Rules apply to moving plants, soil and fruit in and out of the NT. Read [biosecurity and weeds](../biosecurity-and-weeds.md) first, and see the Northern Territory page for declared weeds.

## Gaps

- The sheet is old. Compare it with a local nursery or community garden before you buy a lot of seed.
- Cassava, pigeon pea, cowpea and lablab have no sourced sowing months in this guide.
- No Darwin source on pot sizes, potting mixes or how much to water in pots in the wet and dry seasons. See [small spaces and pots](small-spaces.md).
- Katherine and Darwin share one sheet. No source splits them.
- Alice Springs and Tennant Creek are different climates. This page does not apply to them.
