# Northern Territory

> **How reliable is this page?** The NT is well covered by government crop sheets (Agnotes) and NT Government home-garden pages, but many are old (1997 to 2014) and give months for only a handful of crops. The official Darwin/Katherine sowing grid (Information Sheet IS4) has now been read and is transcribed below; other NT sources are listed after it. The blog-based monthly grid is in a separate table labelled **unverified proxy**, with its conflicts noted. Barkly and Tennant Creek (IS5) and Alice Springs (IS6) grids have not been read yet. Companion planting claims on the NT Government page carry no cited evidence and are treated as folklore.

Onion is not on IS4, so its window stays with Agnote 191. Where sources differ, both are shown and neither is picked.

## Soil, cover crops and rotation

### Top End covers and green manures

- **Grasses first.** The Top End green-manure Agnote says to "go for bulk" with millet or sorghum because soils are low in organic matter. Forage sorghum (for example Jumbo, a nematode-resistant hybrid) and pearl millet (Ingrid, Katherine pearl) smother weeds. Cowpea-type legumes add nitrogen but less organic matter and should not precede nematode-susceptible crops. [NT Agnote 636](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/232968/636.pdf) (government, moderate)
- **Timing and handling (Agnote 636).** Sow November to December. The preferred option is to leave the crop in the ground for 12 months over the dry season; otherwise rotate a crop in straight away. Terminate about end of March in Darwin and about mid-February in Katherine so residue breaks down before vegetables. Do not cart the crop off.
- **Rates in the Agnote are broadacre:** sorghum 20 kg/ha, millet 8 to 12 kg/ha, drill if possible, 150 to 200 kg/ha NPK on virgin soil, first cut at about 2 m leaving 150 to 200 mm stubble, 50 kg/ha urea after each cut. No garden-scale conversion is in the sources reviewed. The Agnote's legume dry-matter figure was garbled in the sources reviewed and is not used.
- **Sunn hemp is weaker.** In one unirrigated Katherine demonstration (2020) sunn hemp gave about 1.1 t/ha dry matter and about 27 kg N/ha, against 6.8 t/ha for pearl millet sown the same day. The authors call it preliminary. [NT Rural Review, Feb 2021](https://industry.nt.gov.au/publications/primary-industry-publications/newsletters/regional-newsletters/rural-review/nt-rural-review-february-2021/sunn-hemp-trial-at-katherine-research-station-results-of-2020-demonstration-site) (government, weak to moderate: single trial). Sunn hemp also hosts guava root-knot nematode (below).
- **Grass-then-vegetables is demonstrated.** A Top End vegetable demonstration used forage sorghum as a green manure in January 2017, incorporated before May vegetable planting. No measured soil results were reported. [Hort Innovation VG15044](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/laserfiche/assets/project-reports/vegnet/vg15044---final-report-complete.pdf) (weak for soil outcomes)
- **Other options.** An NT booklet lists millet as a Katherine green manure trial (2006 to 2007) and mungbean assessed in 1992, with no results given. [NT IB10](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/227569/ib10.pdf) Wet-tropics legume cover rates and inoculant groups exist for Queensland sugarcane country (cowpea, lablab, soybean) but give no Top End biomass or sowing months. [Sugar Research Australia](https://sugarresearch.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Checklist-for-Growing-Legume-Cover-Crops-in-the-Wet-Tropics_D.04.pdf)
- **Wet-season fallow.** IS4 advises fallowing under a thick organic mulch over the wet. The NT manual says to include a green manure crop in the rotation every wet season, and that replanting a second crop into plastic mulch "has never been very successful". [NT Vegetable growing manual](https://industry.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/228007/ntg-vegetable-growing-manual.pdf)

### Sample Top End wet/dry rotation

> **Not a tested prescription.** Adapted from the sourced sorghum-then-vegetables sequence ([Agnote 636](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/232968/636.pdf), [NT manual](https://industry.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/228007/ntg-vegetable-growing-manual.pdf), [VG15044](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/laserfiche/assets/project-reports/vegnet/vg15044---final-report-complete.pdf)). Steps marked [inference] are this guide's own assembly.

1. **November to December (wet season starts):** sow forage sorghum or pearl millet on a vegetable bed, on monsoon rain. A cowpea legume strip is optional but is a nematode-susceptible crop. [inference]
2. **To about end of March (Darwin) or mid-February (Katherine):** cut before seed set and leave the residue as mulch. Do not cart it off.
3. **April to about October (dry season):** vegetable crops with irrigation into the mulch, in the order leafy crop, then beans, then cucurbits (NT manual), using the NT-sourced windows above.
4. **Next wet:** back to a green manure. Use sweet potato or cassava as a long-season break bed. [inference]
5. **Alternative:** leave the green manure in for 12 months over the dry season (Agnote 636's preferred option).

### Nematodes, termites and soil hygiene

- Root-knot nematodes (three species) are well established in the NT, and guava root-knot nematode (GRKN) is concentrated in Darwin's residential suburbs and found in the Top End and Katherine. Hosts include guava, sunn hemp, hay crops and many weeds; galled roots are the sign. Management: hygiene ("come clean, go clean"), clean planting material (do not share tubers, roots or soil), non-host cover crops, weed control in fallows and inter-rows, and good compost may help. Contact plant.pathology@nt.gov.au. [NT DAF, root-knot nematodes](https://daf.nt.gov.au/news/2026/root-knot-nematodes-in-the-nt) (government)
- The NT manual says continuous same-crop planting builds nematodes and soil disease, and legume green manures are susceptible to root-knot so should not precede susceptible vegetables. Use raised beds against Pythium. [NT Vegetable growing manual](https://industry.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/228007/ntg-vegetable-growing-manual.pdf)
- Solarising potting mix with clear plastic worked in Darwin trials against Pythium myriotylum, Phytophthora nicotianae and Sclerotium rolfsii in 3 to 9 days (mix 40 to 60 C). Some Amaranthus and Portulaca still germinated. It worked from September to mid-May; in mid-May to September temperatures were lower. [NT Agnote 537](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/232921/537.pdf) (government, moderate; potting mix only)
- Soils: Top End soils are mainly Kandosols, with cracking Vertosols on floodplains. Central Australian horticulture soils are mainly sandy Kandosols and Tenosols. [NT Soils fact sheet](https://environment.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0016/261061/soils-of-the-nt-factsheet.pdf) (government)
- A four-year NT DAF and University of Queensland soil trial began in December 2024 at six sites across the Top End, Barkly and Central Australia. The stated problems are low organic matter, low fertility, water erosion risk and surface crusting; no results yet. [NT DAF soil trials](https://daf.nt.gov.au/news/2025/soil-research-trials-to-help-territory-growers)
- Sweet potato prefers well-drained sandy loam with high organic matter (avoid heavy clay); use raised beds. [nt.gov.au Sweet potato](https://nt.gov.au/environment/home-gardens/growing-vegetables-at-home/sweet-potato)

### Central Australia soils and water

All from the [Alice Springs Vegie Garden Companion](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/aridlands/pages/4419/attachments/original/1726025047/Vegie_companion_small2.pdf) (community handbook, not peer reviewed) unless stated.

- Soils are typically low in organic matter and nutrients: build with compost, mulch and aged manure. Irrigation raises soil pH over time; use compost and sulphur, avoid lime and dolomite. Salts can build up near the surface; heavy mulch and managed watering help, and a deep watering in late November and again in late January is suggested.
- Mulch at least 7 to 10 cm deep and off stems. Drip is most efficient; about 50% of summer water from microsprays is lost to evaporation. Established mulched gardens can cope with about three waterings a week in summer.
- Gypsum for clay or poorly draining soil, and test pH under citrus. [Alice Springs News, Sept 2020](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2020/09/07/september-is-when-your-garden-needs-you-most/)
- Mulch is the "best water saver"; water first thing in the morning. [Alice Springs Town Council](https://www.alicesprings.nt.gov.au/documents/89/water-saving-tips) (council)
- Shade new seedlings for at least a week in summer; use 50% white shade cloth in extreme heat (avoid green or black, and avoid 70 to 90%); pea straw mulch, not thick sugar-cane mulch. [Alice Springs News, Dec 2020](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2020/11/30/garden-dries-up-in-december-not-if-youre-doing-it-right/)
- Green manures listed: clover, beans, oats, wheat, sorghum, mustard, dug in before flowering. Nematode-resistant options: velvet bean, French marigold, sorghum, crotalaria, white mustard, oats, wheat. Nematodes favour moist warm sandy soils; tomatoes, okra, cucumbers and zucchini are most affected; asparagus, broccoli, cabbage, garlic and onion are more tolerant. Mulberries and figs can keep an infestation going. Compost may cut populations by up to 90% (the guide's claim).
- Cool-season oats, wheat or mustard fit the winter growing window. [inference; the guide gives no timing]
- Sample Central Australian rotation [inference, assembled from the above]: warm-season fruiting crops in September; quick crops in January; oats or mustard as the winter green manure, dug in before flowering; keep tomato, okra and cucurbits off nematode-infested beds.
- For sodic (dispersive) clay, WA's broadacre gypsum table is the only rate guidance in the sources reviewed. It is not garden-scale. See [Western Australia](wa.md) and [DPIRD](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/node/5152). The NT notes found no gypsum guidance for Alice Springs gardens beyond the line above.

- **IS4 month grid (Darwin/Katherine):** now transcribed. It is old (2005/2008), so confirm against a local nursery or the NT Government. IS5 (Tennant Creek) and IS6 (Alice Springs) grids are still unread.
- **No NT months** for cassava, pigeon pea, cowpea, lablab, or green manure sowing beyond the December monsoon advice.
- **Katherine versus Darwin** windows: no Katherine-specific source. The Deep Green January and March posts were not read.
- **No Darwin community garden or council planting calendar** was found (Darwin City Council pamphlets are fruit-tree care only).
- **Barkly and Tennant Creek:** no home-garden source. **Alice Springs frost dates:** none found.
- **Fruit harvest months** for mango (beyond "usually September to December"), pomelo, soursop, custard apple and dragon fruit; NT Agnotes for dragon fruit, mango production, lime, pomelo, soursop, custard apple were not read. Cyclone resilience of fruit trees: not read.
- **Termites in vegetable gardens:** no NT garden-scale barrier guidance; the NT booklet covers trees and uses fipronil. Check the current APVMA permit and label before any treatment.
- **Declared weeds:** check the current list with the Weed Management Branch. The NT plant health manual (soil, seed and home-gardener rules) was not read.
- **Bush foods:** native ginger and a general yam-safety standard were not sourced. Long yam's silence on toxicity is not proof of safety. Most Bininj Kunwok field guide entries would not load.
- **Companion planting:** no NT trials found. **Environmental benefit** of individual crops: nothing quantitative in NT sources.
- **Best places to check:** NT DAF Agnotes (daf.nt.gov.au), NT Government home-garden pages (nt.gov.au), NT Plant Pathology (plant.pathology@nt.gov.au), and the Alice Springs Arid Lands Environment Centre handbook.
