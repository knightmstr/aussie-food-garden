# Queensland

> **How reliable is this page?** Queensland is well sourced for biosecurity rules, avocado care and sugar-industry cover-crop trials. It is weakly sourced for planting months: no Queensland Government or council month-by-month table could be read, so the calendars below come from community, commercial and permaculture sources, and several are proxies for a different climate type. Nothing was found for Western Queensland calendars, and the Granite Belt has one grower blog. Treat every month range as a guide to check locally.

## Soil, cover crops and rotation

### Sugarcane fallow findings (Sugar Research Australia)

These are cane-industry trials in the Wet Tropics. Garden use is an extrapolation [inference].

- **Tully (wet tropics):** soybean, cowpea and lablab gave estimated fresh biomass of 8-32 t/ha. Estimated above-ground N was soybean 70-280, cowpea 55-220 and lablab 45-185 kg/ha. Total N including roots was 90-360, 70-290 and 60-240 kg/ha. Sunn hemp was fast with high biomass but seed was hard to source. Sowing months were not given. Sow into moist soil or when rain is forecast, 25-50 mm deep[243](../references.md#r243). Evidence: moderate (industry R&D, estimates).
- **Rates (drilled):** lablab and cowpea 20-30 kg/ha, soybean 50 kg/ha. Cultivars named for Tully: lablab Rhongi; cowpea Meringa, Ebony, Caloona; soybean Leichardt, Mossman. Other extras named: millet (deep roots), sunflower (can lift phosphorus), mustard, tillage radish (taproot for compaction) [243](../references.md#r243). These are cane-scale rates; no garden-scale rates are sourced.
- **Herbert:** recommended mix rate 30 kg/ha; soil pH should be above 5.5; pre-form mounds to limit waterlogging and Phytophthora/Pythium; grow 3-4 months and terminate before seed set; delay incorporation 2-4 weeks after crimping or slashing to retain N[242](../references.md#r242).
- **Inoculation and nodules:** use fresh seed and match the inoculant group (cowpea I, lablab J, soybean H, sunn hemp M). Check nodules at 4-6 weeks: pink or red inside means fixing[242](../references.md#r242) [244](../references.md#r244).
- **Wet Tropics cane trials (SOIL CRC):** covers gave 5-10 t/ha biomass and suppressed weeds, but no cover reduced pathogenic nematodes there. In drier southern systems, summer covers gave under 2 t/ha and were only tactically useful[201](../references.md#r201).

### Nematode-resistant covers

Root-knot nematode hosts include tomato, capsicum, potato, sweet potato, carrot, eggplant, pumpkin, cucumber, melons, zucchini, lettuce, beans, celery, ginger, strawberry, papaya and banana. Management is rotation with a resistant crop or weed-free bare fallow, resistant varieties, and more organic matter[39](../references.md#r39).

- **Fallow legumes:** lablab is resistant to root-lesion nematode but highly susceptible to root-knot. Cowpea is resistant to root-lesion; Meringa is less susceptible to root-knot. No ratings were given for the other fallow crops[243](../references.md#r243).
- **Glasshouse screening (QDPI, via AUSVEG):** sunn hemp resisted *M. enterolobii*, *M. incognita* and *M. javanica*. Forage sorghum 'Jumbo' and oat 'Swan' were highly resistant to all three. Most soybean varieties and many biofumigants were susceptible, and a mixture works only if every component is resistant. Marigold, cowpea, lablab and brassicas were not assessed there. These are glasshouse results, so check locally[210](../references.md#r210). Evidence: moderate.
- **Banana rotation (north Qld):** brassicas in cool months, sunn hemp in warm months and Rhodes or signal grass for longer fallows cut burrowing nematode by over 95% in the glasshouse. Some commercial seed blends contain nematode hosts, so mix your own from resistant species[206](../references.md#r206).
- **Sunn hemp elsewhere (NT, not Queensland):** one unirrigated Katherine trial gave poor biomass, and guava root-knot nematode infects sunn hemp in the NT[137](../references.md#r137) [117](../references.md#r117). The Queensland screening and the NT findings differ in place and species. Neither resolves the other.

### Green manures for south-east gardens

- **Warm season:** buckwheat, cowpea, French white millet, Japanese millet, lablab, marigolds, mung bean, soybean. **Cool season:** fava/broad bean, tick bean, fenugreek, lupins, oats, subclover, woolly pod vetch, ryecorn, mustards. In warmer cool-season areas such as southern Queensland, sow April-May. Cut at the onset of flowering, then wait 4-6 weeks after digging in before planting[270](../references.md#r270). Evidence: moderate (Green Harvest/SGA lists).
- No SEQ-specific Queensland Government green manure page was found. Pigeon pea, crotalaria and lupin in subtropical gardens are unverified. Sunn hemp's crotalaria seed toxicity and weediness were not researched.

### Sample rotation (inference)

This 4-season sequence is this guide's own synthesis, not a tested prescription [inference]:

1. **Summer:** sunn hemp, sorghum or a cowpea mix on beds that held tomato, cucurbits or sweet potato, especially if root-knot is present.
2. **Autumn (Mar-May):** brassicas, greens and onions after a cut-and-mulch legume; oats ('Swan' where root-knot is present) or broad bean on resting beds.
3. **Winter (Jun-Aug):** peas, broad beans, carrots, beetroot, lettuce.
4. **Spring (Sep-Nov):** tomato, capsicum, cucumber, zucchini, corn after the winter legume or oat cover, leaving 4-6 weeks after dig-in.

Rotate plant families and avoid following a root-knot host with another host. The Sustainable Gardening Australia (SGA) four-bed cycle is legume, heavy feeder, light feeder, green manure[267](../references.md#r267). More on rotation is on the [Principles](../principles.md) page.

### State soil problems

- **Acidity:** caused by high ammonium-N fertiliser on naturally acidic soils, nitrate leaching and removal of plant material. Use less acidifying practices and agricultural lime, and treat early because subsoil acidity is hard and costly to fix. The page gives no pH targets[160](../references.md#r160). TAFE Queensland suggests pH about 6-6.5 for summer vegetables[166](../references.md#r166). Herbert cane guidance says above 5.5 and that lime can take up to four months to shift pH[242](../references.md#r242). These targets differ by use, so soil test first.
- **Heavy wet-season rain:** raise beds where drainage is poor; Dayboro lost a carrot bed one February[255](../references.md#r255). Bare fallow reduces nematodes but raises erosion risk[210](../references.md#r210).
- **Cooktown:** local soils generally poor; soil biology is slow in the dry; get an independent lab soil test, mound shallow planting sites and build organic matter with ground covers and mulch[178](../references.md#r178).
- **Western Queensland:** Barcaldine soil is "sandy, hungry, alkaline and infertile", and termites do the soil work earthworms do elsewhere[369](../references.md#r369). No fixes were sourced. Cover crops in semi-arid areas use subsoil water and gave limited biomass in dry southern systems[201](../references.md#r201).
- **Liming rates, compaction and erosion figures for gardens:** not sourced.

## Fruit and nut trees here

The national table is on [Fruit and nut trees](../fruit-and-nut-trees.md).

- **Avocado:** drainage matters most. Allow at least 2 m of well-drained soil in high rainfall (1.5 m in lower rainfall), mound rows with 2-5% fall, avoid windy sites and prefer frost-free sites (mature trees tolerate about -4 C briefly, but not with flowers or fruit). Flowering needs about 4 weeks of relatively cool autumn and winter temperatures. Hass flowering north of about Yeppoon is generally unsatisfactory. Fruit set needs 3 consecutive days at 10 C or above. Irrigation is essential in most of Queensland from August to April, and Phytophthora root rot is the key disease[34](../references.md#r34). Evidence: strong (government page). Planting time and spacing were not read.
- **Brisbane tree list (nursery source, weak-moderate):** lemon, lime (frost-sensitive), mandarin, mango (avoid excess nitrogen), lychee (wind shelter), guava, finger lime, mulberry, loquat, avocado (mound), dragon fruit, low-chill peach and nectarine[307](../references.md#r307).
- **Cooktown:** acerola, carambola, custard apple, sugar apple, sapodilla, macadamia, native guava, citrus, banana and papaya. The handbook says mixing fruit species rather than monoculture reduces pest damage (design-handbook claim, not experimental) [178](../references.md#r178).
- **Citrus (national sources, not Queensland-specific):** in tropical and subtropical regions plant in winter; oranges and grapefruit tolerate frost better than lemons and limes[339](../references.md#r339). A nursery source says spring when soil is about 16 C[299](../references.md#r299). The two sources differ, so choose by local frost risk.
- **Mango (Northern Territory source, a proxy):** needs a dry, cool spell to flower and warm, rainless conditions while fruiting, and no frost[133](../references.md#r133).
- **Pollination:** stingless bees pollinate macadamia, lychee, mango and avocado[223](../references.md#r223). Business Queensland suggests 2-10 honey bee hives per hectare if pollinators are scarce in avocado orchards[34](../references.md#r34).
- **Chill for the cooler districts:** the national fact sheet gives 10th-percentile winter chill of 69 portions at Applethorpe, Qld (Applethorpe is on the Granite Belt [general knowledge]). That compares with 91 at Lenswood SA and 105 at Huonville Tas[240](../references.md#r240). No Granite Belt orchard source was read.
- **Not sourced for Queensland:** planting windows or frost data for mango, lychee, macadamia, custard apple, passionfruit, pineapple, papaya and banana care.

## Pests and biosecurity here

See [insect pests](../pests/insects.md), [diseases, weeds and soil problems](../pests/diseases.md) and [Biosecurity and weeds](../biosecurity-and-weeds.md).

> **Biosecurity:** Report suspect Panama TR4 within 24 hours on 13 25 23 or 1800 084 881. Do not cut a suspect plant open to look inside[35](../references.md#r35).

### Panama TR4 and banana movement

- Panama TR4 is a soil-borne fungal disease that survives decades in soil. It was found in the Tully Valley, FNQ, with movement restrictions on a small number of properties. Subtropical race 4 occurs in SEQ within the southern banana biosecurity zone, and races 1 and 2 are present in Queensland. Fruit is not infected[35](../references.md#r35).
- It spreads via infected plants and suckers, soil and water on vehicles, tools and footwear, banana weevil borer and pigs. It is category 1 restricted matter, and backyard bananas would be affected. Buy plants only from accredited suppliers[35](../references.md#r35).
- A biosecurity certificate is needed to move banana plants (not fruit), their soil or machinery out of any banana zone, or into the Northern Banana Biosecurity Zone[36](../references.md#r36). Avoid swapping suckers between districts [inference].

### Fire ants, electric ants and zones

- In fire ant zones, soil is treated as high risk. A permit is needed to move soil from zone 2 to zone 1, or to outside the zones.
- Electric ant zone carriers include soil, potted plants, cuttings, fruit, turf, mulch, baled hay or straw, compost and manure.
- Papaya plants need a certificate to leave Papaya Ringspot Zone 1 in SEQ. Cucurbit plants need a permit to leave Papaya Ringspot Zone 2. Plants and soil leaving the far-northern Cape York zones need a permit, and sugarcane zones need certificates.
- Apply through 13 25 23. All of the above is from Business Qld: moving within Queensland[36](../references.md#r36). Evidence: strong (government). That page does not cover citrus canker, banana freckle or fruit fly.

### Queensland fruit fly (home garden)

- Pick ripe fruit before it falls, and collect fallen fruit in sealed plastic bags left in the sun for 3-7 days. Prune trees to pickable height and remove spent clivia flower heads. Bananas and avocados can be picked mature-green before the fly lays eggs. Use pheromone traps (also for detection), and chickens scratching can help with pupae. Hosts include peach, orange, apple, pear, tomato and capsicum[273](../references.md#r273). Netting and baits are not covered on that page.
- Fruit fly is a pressure point in summer in Central Queensland, and Davidson plum (*D. jerseyana*) is susceptible[328](../references.md#r328) [269](../references.md#r269).

### Root-knot nematode

Galls of 1-10 mm and patchy stunting or wilt are the signs. "Come clean, go clean" hygiene for nematodes and TR4 are parallel practices [inference]. Details are in the soil section above.

### Weeds and restricted plants

- Queensland's restricted invasive plants are regulated under Schedule 1A of the Biosecurity Regulation 2016, and the general biosecurity obligation applies to everyone. The page lists cat's claw creeper and lantana among others, but it did not give a full list and does not list leucaena, pigeon pea or castor oil[37](../references.md#r37).
- A second Business Queensland page, at a different address, says Queensland lists similar *Senna*, *Mimosa*, mesquite and prickly acacia[38](../references.md#r38).
- Absence from a list is not clearance. Whether pigeon pea, sunn hemp, lablab, castor oil or leucaena are restricted was not confirmed, and local government pest plant lists were not read. Leucaena has a published Queensland weed-significance assessment that was not read[44](../references.md#r44).

## Gaps and what to check locally

- **Calendars:** no Business Queensland, DAF, Brisbane City Council or Growing Queensland month table. Check your local council gardening page and Business Queensland.
- **Townsville, Mackay and Rockhampton:** no calendar and no frost or rain data. Ask a local council or community garden.
- **Toowoomba and Darling Downs:** nothing read. Granite Belt has one blog and no orchard source.
- **Western Queensland:** no calendar, no soil-building source beyond one Barcaldine description.
- **Cover crops:** no sowing months in either SRA guide; no SEQ green manure page; no garden-scale rates.
- **Fruit trees:** no planting window or frost data for mango, lychee, macadamia, custard apple, passionfruit, pineapple, papaya or citrus from the Queensland Government.
- **Biosecurity:** citrus canker, banana freckle, papaya ringspot detail and Queensland fruit fly zone status were not retrieved. Check Business Queensland or call 13 25 23.
- **Declared status** of pigeon pea, lablab, sunn hemp, castor oil and leucaena: check Business Queensland and your local council pest plant list.
- **Native foods:** nothing read on riberry, native ginger or myrtle rust in Queensland, and no Indigenous nursery ownership verified.
- **Companion planting:** few Queensland-specific claims have evidence.
- **Unread pages worth checking:** Deep Green's other subtropical months, Gardenate month pages, other Sunshine Coast Council months.
