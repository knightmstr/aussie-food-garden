# Victoria

> **How reliable is this page?** The best-sourced parts are biosecurity (Agriculture Victoria on Queensland fruit fly, anthracnose and sodosols), clubroot and green-manure guidance, and one Victorian orchard-floor trial. The planting calendars are weak: no government month-by-month calendar for Melbourne, central Victoria, Gippsland or the Mallee could be read, so the tables below come from commercial sellers, a council blog, community sites and a local newspaper, and several are proxies for other places. Where sources disagree, both are shown. Frost dates for Victoria are not sourced from the Bureau of Meteorology (BoM); check local BoM climate data before you plant frost-tender crops.

## Soil, cover crops and rotation

### Covers and green manures

- Cool-season species: broad (fava) beans, tick beans, fenugreek, lupins, oats, subclover, woolly pod vetch, rye, yellow and black mustard, other brassicas, wheat, barley. Warm-season species: buckwheat, cowpea, millets, lablab, marigolds, mung bean, soybean. In cool-winter areas (Victoria is named) sow cool-season covers in early autumn, or after autumn rain if dry; early spring also works. Warm-season covers in colder areas go in after frost. [SGA green manure](https://sgaonline.org.au/green-manure) (community/practitioner)
- Cut or dig in at the start of flowering. Chop first and wait 4 to 6 weeks before sowing, or leave as surface mulch. Biofumigant brassicas must be dug in to release their gases. [SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/green-manure)
- Do not let seed-setting covers drop seed in the garden (weed risk). [inference]
- Residues: microbes work best around 24:1 carbon to nitrogen. Oat straw is about 70:1 and cereal rye straw about 82:1, so they can tie up nitrogen; forage radish and mustards are about 15:1 and break down fast. [AUSVEG/SoilWealth](https://ausveg.com.au/app/uploads/2024/09/ManagingCoverCropResiduesSOILWEALTHwebversion.pdf) (industry)
- Vineyard mid-row trials (NSW, Hunter Valley and Orange, not Victoria): field pea gave the highest dry matter and nitrogen fixation; faba bean gave good biomass and strongly attracted bees; lupins failed because they were sown after the recommended early April. Rates (field pea 100, faba 180, crimson clover 8 to 10 kg/ha) are vineyard rates, not garden rates. [Mid-row crops for vineyards](https://dpird-web.matrix.squiz.cloud/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/1158130/Mid-row-crops-for-vineyards.pdf) (government-hosted)
- Victorian apple and pear orchard floors (Hort Innovation AP00033, trials 2001-03 at Mooroopna North, Silvan, Templestowe and Red Hill): buckwheat and chicory/yarrow were marginally best for yield and damage. Mustard plots had the most russet at Mooroopna North, and European red mite overwintered more under mustard, ryegrass/fescue and volunteer treatments at Silvan. Establishment was difficult and drought forced site abandonment. Evidence: moderate, results mixed. [Hort Innovation AP00033](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/historic-reports/orchard-habitat-management-to-enhance-ipm-systems-in-pome-fruit-ap00033.pdf)
- In the same study, swamp foxtail, canola, lucerne, vetch, calendula, nasturtium, lobelia, cotula and viola were poor light brown apple moth hosts; strawberry, azalea and veronica were good hosts and white clover and dichondra marginal. Under-planting apple trees with strawberry or white clover may therefore raise light brown apple moth risk in Victoria. [inference] (the host test was in a glasshouse). Same source.

### Rotation

- SGA's family groups: Brassicaceae, Solanaceae, Cucurbitaceae, Amaranthaceae, Apiaceae, Alliaceae, Fabaceae, Asteraceae, Poaceae. Rules: no same-family crops back to back, no heavy feeder after a heavy feeder, a green manure at least once every four seasons. Cycle: legume, then heavy feeder, then light feeder, then green manure, with beds staggered. The page has internal inconsistencies (for example lettuce is listed as both daisy family and heavy feeder). [SGA crop rotation](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/crop-rotation/)
- Sample temperate rotation [inference, adapted from SGA's cycle]: Bed 1 autumn broad bean or pea; Bed 2 tomato or pumpkin the following spring; Bed 3 carrot, beetroot or onion; Bed 4 oats or buckwheat green manure, cut before seed set. Brassicas get their own long, clubroot-aware cycle.

### Soil problems

- **Clubroot (brassicas).** Spores persist up to 20 years. Keep soil at pH 7.0 to 7.5 with lime, applied at least a week before transplanting; below pH 6 raises risk (evidence: strong, Hort Innovation, Werribee Victoria trials). Calcium nitrate and boron inhibit infection (moderate). Brassica cover crops and biofumigants can raise risk in infected beds. Control host weeds such as shepherd's purse and wild radish. [Hort Innovation VG15010](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-clubroot-management-in-brassica-vegetables-fact-sheet.pdf)
- **Sodosols (clay subsoils, dispersive).** Management: gypsum, more surface organic matter, reduced cultivation and residue retention, work soil at optimum moisture, raised beds and drainage, avoid hardpans. [Agriculture Victoria VRO](https://vro.agriculture.vic.gov.au/DPI/Vro/vrosite.nsf/pages/hort_vic_soils-sodosol) (government). Gypsum helps sodic, dispersive clays only, and the rate comes from a lab test. [CCMA Dispersive Soils note](https://soilhealth.ccmaknowledgebase.vic.gov.au/SoilHealthResource/documents/outputs/education_and_training/Dispersive%20Soils%20-%20Agriculture%20Note.pdf) (government). Apply gypsum and lime after a soil test, not by default. [inference]
- **Acidity.** GRDC's broadacre target is pH 5.5 to 6.0 (CaCl2), and lime above about 2 t/ha on sand risks micronutrient deficiency. That is a broadacre figure and does not transfer to vegetable beds, which the clubroot sheet puts higher for brassicas. [GRDC 2022](https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/grdc-update-papers/tab-content/grdc-update-papers/2022/02/removing-yield-barriers-caused-by-acidity-and-preventing-subsoil-acidification)

## Fruit and nut trees here

For the national table see [Fruit and nut trees](../fruit-and-nut-trees.md).

- Victoria produced 43% of Australian apples in 2015-16, mainly in the Goulburn Valley, Yarra Valley, Harcourt and southern Victoria (Mornington Peninsula, Gippsland). Main varieties: Cripps Pink, Granny Smith, Royal Gala. [Australian Apples (APAL)](https://www.aussieapples.com.au/?p=11) (industry body)
- Winter chill is the key selector. 10th-percentile winter chill in chill portions (1 March to 31 August, 1911-2012): Tatura 81 and Yarra Valley 94 (Victoria), against Spreyton 94 and Huonville 105 (Tasmania). The sheet recommends the Dynamic model (chill portions) over the older chill-hours count, and the two cannot be directly converted. [Horticulture Innovation winter chill fact sheet](https://hin.com.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/18265/Horticulture-winter-chill-fact-sheet-FINAL.pdf) (industry)
- Apple: winter planting while dormant (bare-root gives the widest choice); most are self-sterile, so plant two compatible varieties or a multi-grafted tree; first crop about 2 to 3 years. Stone fruit: winter planting while dormant; late frosts can destroy blossom, so choose late-flowering varieties and avoid frost-prone gullies; cherries need the most chill; plums and most cherries need a second variety. [SBS apple](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/how-to-grow-apple/3iv754eo4); [SBS stone fruit](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/how-to-grow-stone-fruit/4kk0te6sv) (media, moderate)
- Nuts (NSW DPI industry factsheets, so not Victoria-specific): chestnut suits areas including south to Shepparton, needs 58 to 88 chill portions by cultivar and is wind pollinated, so plant several cultivars. Hazelnut needs 48 to 60 chill portions and late spring frosts damage female flowers. Walnut: the factsheet's text (48 to 60 portions) and its table (54 to 72) disagree. [NSW DPI chestnut](https://dpird-web.matrix.squiz.cloud/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/586487/chestnut-industry-expansion.pdf); [NSW DPI hazelnut](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/586506/hazelnut-industry-expansion.pdf); [NSW DPI walnut](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/586590/walnut-industry-expansion.pdf) (government, other state)
- Quince (NSW DPI): needs some winter chilling; avoid valley floors and frost pockets; plant late June to early July when dormant; prune in winter. [NSW DPI quince](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/agriculture/horticulture/pomes/other/quince-growing)
- Codling moth (PIRSA, South Australia, so a proxy): corrugated cardboard trunk bands checked every 3 weeks, paling traps, clearing loose bark, and removing fallen fruit; breeding starts above about 15 C average. [PIRSA codling moth](https://pir.sa.gov.au/crops-and-plants/weeds-and-plant-pests/plant-pests/common-seasonal-pests/codling_moth)
- Not sourced for Victoria: pruning and planting windows for nuts, berries, grapes, kiwiberry; pollination partners by variety.

### Queensland fruit fly (home gardens)

Queensland fruit fly is found throughout Victoria. Home gardeners are expected to manage it and need not report it. Populations usually rise in spring and can stay active into autumn and winter in warm weather. [Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/biosecurity/pest-insects-and-mites/priority-pest-insects-and-mites/queensland-fruit-fly/controlling-queensland-fruit-fly-in-home-gardens) (government)

1. **Net fruit trees.** Use fine, UV-stable insect-proof mesh over a frame after pollination, secured at the trunk and keeping fruit off the mesh (the fly can sting through it). The sources reviewed do not give a mesh size. Bag or sleeve individual fruit as an alternative. (Evidence: strong.)
2. **Trap.** Hang traps about 1.5 m high in shaded foliage, set up 6 to 8 weeks before fruit ripens. Types: para-pheromone (males), protein (both sexes) and fruit-scent. A homemade bottle trap uses 3 cups juice and 3 tbsp cloudy ammonia, changed weekly; keep it from pets and children. Traps alone are unlikely to manage the fly, and they can catch beneficial insects. (Traps alone: weak.) [Agriculture Victoria PDF](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/532139/Managing-Queensland-fruit-fly-in-your-home-garden.pdf)
3. **Pick and clear.** Pick ripe produce promptly and clear fallen fruit.
4. **Kill maggots.** Microwave, boil, freeze in sealed bags for two days, or seal in two bags in the sun for at least 14 days. Do not compost infested fruit.
5. **Bait.** Apply bait sprays to foliage, not fruit. Spinosad bait (Naturalure) is the organic-certified option for fruiting vegetables, reapplied weekly because sunlight breaks it down. Avoid flowering plants with active bees. Evidence: moderate. [VG15010 biopesticides](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/resource-assets/vg15010-biopesticides-in-australia-fact-sheet.pdf); [PIRSA Naturalure technote](https://pir.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/497543/Naturalure-Bee-Safety-Technote.pdf). Check the label for registration and use.

- Mediterranean fruit fly: Victoria is free. It infests more than 200 plant species including fruiting vegetables. Do not carry fruit or fruiting vegetables from South Australia or Western Australia into Victoria. [Agriculture Victoria Medfly](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/biosecurity/pest-insects-and-mites/priority-pest-insects-and-mites/mediterranean-fruit-fly)
- Netting and wildlife: wildlife-unsafe netting is being phased out or banned in some states. This comes from search snippets only; check current Victorian law and the [Wildlife Victoria fruit tree page](https://www.wildlife.vic.gov.au/managing-wildlife/wildlife-and-fruit-trees). (Weak.)

### Diseases and other pests

- **Bean anthracnose:** angular reddish-brown spots along veins and dark sunken lesions on stems and pods. Infected seed is the main route; rain, overhead irrigation and wind spread it. Use certified or disease-free seed, avoid beans for at least 2 years after an infected crop, and do not work wet infected crops. Tweed Wonder, Wellington Wonder and Redlands Beauty resist the most common race but can be badly hit by other races. (Strong, government.) [Agriculture Victoria](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/biosecurity/plant-diseases/vegetable-diseases/anthracnose-of-beans)
- **Late blight (potato, tomato):** recent outbreaks are concentrated in northern Tasmania and in Victoria (Gippsland, Central Highlands, Otways) and Mt Gambier. Infected tubers and volunteer potatoes are the main inoculum. Kangaroo apple and nightshades act as indicator weeds. [AUSVEG PT04010](https://ausveg.com.au/app/data/technical-insights/docs/PT04010.pdf)
- **Clubroot:** see the soil section above.
- **Council IPM guide (Yarra Ranges):** names beneficial insects and the plants that attract them (feverfew, parsley, alyssum, yarrow), and says kaolin acts as a barrier against codling moth. Its Trichogramma and kaolin claims are from the council page; Australian registration of kaolin for home use is unverified. [Yarra Ranges Council IPM guide](https://www.yarraranges.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/webdocuments/environment/sustainable-home-garden/natural-pest-control-using-integrated-pest-management-principles-1.pdf) (council)

- **Soil:** no Victorian soil-type guidance beyond sodosols and clubroot liming (volcanic plains and Gippsland soils are unsourced). Get a soil test.
- **Native foods:** native raspberry, karkalla or pigface, lilly pilly and Victorian-specific Aboriginal-led murnong projects were not verified.
