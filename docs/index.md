# Aussie Food Garden

A sourced guide to growing food in Australia in ways that also look after the local environment: what to plant and when, what grows well together, what to rotate or sow to keep soil fertile, and how to deal with pests and disease organically. It is organised by state and territory, with shared pages for rotation, pests, fruit trees, native foods and biosecurity.

> **How much to trust it:** every fact links to the page it came from, and anything that does not is tagged `[inference]` or `[general knowledge]`. Many official planting calendars are images or PDF grids that could not be read, so some regions have thin or proxy data. Each page says exactly what is weak. Follow product labels, and check local rules before moving plants or fruit. More in [Sources, limits and updates](about.md).

## What do you need?

- **I want to know what to plant now:** open the app's Planting calendar for Darwin or Sydney, or your state page and go to *What to plant, month by month*. Check the source type beside each table.
- **Aphids are on my plants:** start with [aphids](pests/insects.md#aphids), then the [pest finder](pests/insects.md#pest-finder) for other insects.
- **Something is wrong with the leaves or soil:** use the [problem finder](pests/diseases.md#problem-finder). [Powdery mildew](pests/diseases.md#powdery-mildew), [clubroot](pests/diseases.md#clubroot) and [root-knot nematode](pests/diseases.md#root-knot-nematode) are covered.
- **I want soil that stays fertile:** read [rotation, soil and companions](principles.md), which separates what has evidence from what is folklore.
- **I want fruit or nut trees:** see [fruit and nut trees](fruit-and-nut-trees.md).
- **I want native foods:** read the safety and sourcing advice first in [native foods](native-foods.md).
- **I am moving plants, soil or fruit, or planting something that spreads:** see [biosecurity and weeds](biosecurity-and-weeds.md).

## Ground rules used throughout

1. **Cite it or tag it.** No invented months, rates, product names or registration status.
2. **Evidence is graded.** *Strong* means a government or university trial or label. *Weak* means a practitioner, blog or folklore. Where sources disagree, both are shown.
3. **Organic does not mean harmless.** Soaps, oils, copper and sulphur can hurt bees, beneficials and plants if misused. Use them exactly as the label says.
4. **Native foods are not for wild harvest.** Buy from Indigenous-led suppliers where you can.

## Use it on your phone

This guide is also a small offline web app. Open its link, then use *Add to Home Screen* (iPhone, in Safari) or *Install app* (Android, in Chrome). It saves pages as you read them, so it works without signal. The setup steps are in `PUBLISHING.md` in the repository.
