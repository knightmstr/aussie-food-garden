# Fruit and nut trees

> **How reliable is this page?** Only trees with at least one sourced fact have a row, and many common trees (pineapple, lychee care, persimmon, almond, kiwifruit and most berries) have none; see [Gaps](#gaps). The best sourced trees are mango, papaya, banana, avocado, citrus (partly from commercial and media sources), the NSW DPI nuts and quince, and the winter chill figures. Most planting months are missing because the source pages did not give them, and several pages are thin, dated or from one grower. Nothing here replaces your state agency's local advice.

## How to read this page

- **Chill hours versus chill portions.** Deciduous fruit and nut trees need winter cold. The older chill-hours model counts hours between 0 and 7.2 C. The Dynamic model counts chill portions and is called current best practice in the Horticulture Innovation winter chill fact sheet. The two cannot be directly converted ([Horticulture Innovation fact sheet](https://hin.com.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/18265/Horticulture-winter-chill-fact-sheet-FINAL.pdf)). Nursery and media pages in this page use hours below about 7 C; the nut fact sheets use portions. Do not compare one with the other.
- **Reliability labels** in the last column are this guide's judgement of the source type, not a trial grade. Strong: a government or industry fact sheet with specifics. Moderate: a government page that is thin, or a commercial or community page with specifics. Weak: media, a blog, a single grower's talk, forum comments or a proxy for another place.
- Where the sources reviewed disagree, both statements are shown and the conflict is flagged.
- "Not sourced" means the sources reviewed held nothing. State pages with local detail: [Northern Territory](states/nt.md), [Queensland](states/qld.md), [NSW and ACT](states/nsw-act.md), [Western Australia](states/wa.md), [South Australia](states/sa.md), [Victoria](states/vic.md), [Tasmania](states/tas.md).

- **Mango disagreements.** One note reports NT mango harvest as usually September to December from the home garden page; another note found no mango planting or harvest months on the NT grower page and could not read the NT fruit availability grid. Treat the months as unconfirmed. The home garden page says fruit-bearing trees need water from flowering until fruit development ends; the grower page says from fruit set.
- **Mango movement and pruning.** Mango plants moved within the NT must be treated for mango leaf hopper, and fruit treated six weeks before harvest (Kensington Pride, Calypso and Honey Gold exempt). Mango flowers on mature wood, so hard pruning can delay fruiting 2-3 years ([NT grower advice](https://nt.gov.au/industry/agriculture/food-crops-plants-and-quarantine/fruit-crops/mango/grower-advice); [NT travelling within the NT](https://nt.gov.au/industry/agriculture/food-crops-plants-and-quarantine/plants-and-quarantine/travelling-within-the-nt)).
- **Jackfruit flag.** The NT page says jackfruit needs no pronounced dry season, which sits oddly with Darwin's long dry season. The sources reviewed advise checking this before giving advice.
- **Establishing an orchard (NT Agnote D12, 2002).** Most species (including citrus, guava and mango) are best planted in the early wet season, and mango any time if irrigation is running. Plant on overcast days, check for termites before clearing and treat planting holes, net ripening fruit of several species, fence against wallabies, pigs and buffalo, keep mulch 5-10 cm from the stem, build windbreaks early, and treat water supply as the key site criterion. Its termite chemical advice comes from the 2002 text; follow the current product label or permit ([NT Agnote D12](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/232942/579.pdf), government, dated). Giant termites (Mastotermes) are a major pest of tree crops and often ringbark ([NT Agnote A14](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/232922/332.pdf)).
- **Pruning rule from the NT pages.** Prune guava and mango after harvest, and avoid hard cutting of mango.
- **Mixed orchards.** A Cooktown design handbook lists acerola, carambola, custard apple, sugar apple, sapodilla, macadamia, native guava, citrus, banana and papaya, and suggests mixing fruit species rather than monoculture to reduce pest damage (design advice, not experimental evidence; [Cook Shire guide](https://www.cook.qld.gov.au/community/sustainable-living-1/NoMowerFoodGrowersGuide.pdf/@@download/file/NoMowerFoodGrowersGuide.pdf)). A northern NSW tourist orchard says crop diversity helps prevent pest build-up, nets or manages Queensland fruit fly rather than spraying, and bags bananas against fruit bats (a tourism article, not a growing guide, weak; [Tropical Fruit World](https://growgreatfruit.com/tropical-fruit-world/)).
- **Fruit availability.** The NT availability page marks papaya, banana, lemon, lime and passionfruit for all 12 months in Darwin and Katherine; exact months for mango, pomelo, soursop, custard apple and dragon fruit were unreadable ([NT fruit availability](https://nt.gov.au/industry/agriculture/food-crops-plants-and-quarantine/fruit-crops/fruit-availability-and-storage)).
- **Guava and nematodes.** Guava is the preferred host of guava root-knot nematode, so avoid it on nematode-prone Darwin sites ([NT DAF](https://daf.nt.gov.au/news/2026/root-knot-nematodes-in-the-nt)).
- [inference] Because mango needs a dry, cool spell to flower, the likely local concerns are wet-season planting and irrigation planning. No source stated an NT planting time.

For south-east Queensland and northern NSW.

| Lychee | Needs wind shelter | Not sourced | Not sourced | [Fruitopia Nursery](https://fruitopianursery.com.au/blogs/news/best-fruit-trees-for-brisbane) (commercial, weak) |
| Finger lime (native) | Border ranges rainforest of SE Qld and northern NSW; understorey shrub or tree up to 6 m | Frost, water and pollination details not on the page read | Not sourced | [NSW DPI finger limes](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/agriculture/horticulture/citrus/content/manuals-guides/finger-limes) (government, thin) |

- **Avocado detail.** Fruit set needs 3 consecutive days at 10 C or higher at flowering. Hass flowering in coastal areas north of about Yeppoon is generally not satisfactory. Mature trees tolerate short periods to 40 C but fruit damage is expected. Poor drainage is a problem and a raised mound helps (Fruitopia). If bee activity is low, use 2-10 hives per hectare, introduced when about 10% of flowers are open. Prune Hass one major limb a year; upright varieties such as Gem and Reed are topped every 2-3 years. Water salinity should be 0.6 dS/m or less and chloride 80 mg/L or less. Planting time and fertiliser were not on the page read. [inference] Because Hass needs about 4 cool weeks to flower, other varieties may suit warmer coastal sites; this is not a variety recommendation.
- **Citrus detail (Diggers, commercial).** Feed when soil is at least 13 C and moist, monthly with citrus fertiliser from a month after planting; prune annually after harvest once frost risk has passed; remove flowers and fruit on young trees for the first few years; overwatering and poor drainage kill more citrus than drought; keep mulch off the trunk. Cut off and burn trees' gall wasp-infected branches; never compost them.
- **Citrus detail (SBS, media).** Water once or twice a week in year one, then deeply every 2-3 weeks; feed in July, November and February; remove up to 20% of the canopy a year after harvest. Grafted trees crop in 2-3 years (the page also says 3), seedlings take 7 or more years and may not come true. The two sources give slightly different feeding starts (Diggers: a month after planting, or a seaweed tonic at planting). Oranges and grapefruit tolerate frost better than lemons and limes; citrus grows in all parts of Australia except severe-frost areas.
- **Brisbane tree list (commercial nursery, weak).** Lemon needs drainage and full sun; lime is frost-sensitive; mango should not get excess nitrogen; guava, mandarin, mulberry, loquat, dragon fruit (needs support) and low-chill peach and nectarine (low winter chill limits) are also listed as suited. Humid summers raise fungal problems where airflow is poor. September is named for planting evergreen trees including citrus and passionfruit ([Deep Green subtropical September](https://deepgreenpermaculture.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/GardenCalendar_Subtropical_September.pdf), commercial).
- **Stingless bees.** Macadamia, lychee, mango, avocado and watermelon are pollinated by stingless bees (Tetragonula carbonaria ranges from coastal Queensland south to Bega, NSW), and blueberry by native bees ([BeeAware](https://beeaware.org.au/pollination/native-bees/native-bees-as-alternative-pollinators/)).
- **Persimmon, fig and loquat** in the subtropics: no care text was obtained. Loquat appears only as a winter fruit fly shelter ([NSW DPI Qfly](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/dpi/biosecurity/invasive-plants-and-animals/insect-pests/queensland-fruit-fly)).

[inference] Stop fruit dropping and do not dump green waste, since birds spread seed. A Flinders Ranges quandong orchard interplanted with olives is a single anecdote ([Landscape SA](https://www.landscape.sa.gov.au/saal/news/whats-involved-in-managing-a-quandong-orchard)).

- **Pistachio caution.** The paper is one grower's talk from the 2000s. It also notes the crop "killer" is the long wait for a return, and it is a long-horizon crop. [inference] Treat these figures as indicative.
- Almond, citrus in a Mediterranean setting, grape and persimmon: no sourced care row (see [Gaps](#gaps)).

For Victoria, Tasmania, the SA hills, southern NSW highlands and the Perth hills.

- **Apple detail (SBS).** Deep soak at least fortnightly without rain and mulch well; feed in early spring, summer and autumn once cropping; gypsum yearly for calcium; summer-pinch inward growth on young trees. 'Jonathon' is described as a widely compatible pollinator for cool-climate varieties. Regular bearing at 3-4 years, peak around year 6. Bare-root trees give the widest choice; potted trees can go in at other times if roots are undisturbed.
- **Stone fruit detail (SBS).** Cut laterals right after harvest; for varieties prone to bacterial canker, delay pruning until spring just before leaf. Deep soak about weekly when dry, mulched. Choose low-chill varieties in warm areas and late-flowering varieties; avoid frost-prone gullies. Time to first crop is not stated.
- **Cherry and pruning timing (Hobart nursery, commercial).** Prune apple, pear, quince and nuts in winter; prune cherry immediately after summer fruiting; copper spray at bud swell and again at about 80% petal fall, repeated about every 2 weeks in wet Tasmanian springs. Follow the product label or permit for any copper spray, and avoid spraying open flowers where bees are working [general knowledge].
- **Nut pruning and planting windows** were not given by the NSW DPI fact sheets or Daleys.
- **Orchard understorey.** In a Victorian trial buckwheat and chicory/yarrow were marginally best; mustard raised russet and mite numbers; results were mixed ([Hort Innovation AP00033](https://www.horticulture.com.au/globalassets/hort-innovation/historic-reports/orchard-habitat-management-to-enhance-ipm-systems-in-pome-fruit-ap00033.pdf)). Comfrey and daffodil "guild" roles are folklore in the sources read (no evidence cited).
- **Where apples are grown.** Victoria produced 43% of Australian apples in 2015-16 (Goulburn Valley, Yarra Valley, Harcourt, Mornington Peninsula, Gippsland); Tasmania's apples are 83% from the Huon, and Tasmania has Area Freedom status for fruit fly; Batlow and Orange NSW suit apples ([APAL](https://www.aussieapples.com.au/?p=11), industry).

A Tasmanian Institute fact sheet says Tasmania achieves higher chill than other states, up to 40% more than WA ([UTAS dormancy fact sheet](https://www.utas.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/1662213/FS_DoA_dormancy.pdf), university).
- A Queensland chill and thermal time calculator for Australia exists (https://grf-smartfarm.dpi.qld.gov.au/shiny/apps/chillcalculator/) but was not opened for these notes.

**Nut chill (NSW DPI).** Hazelnut 48-60 portions (dormancy May-October, cultivar dependent). Chestnut 58-88 portions by cultivar. Walnut: **the fact sheet contradicts itself**, with the text saying 48-60 portions and Table 1 saying 54-72. Both are shown and the conflict is not resolved here; check with NSW DPI before choosing a walnut cultivar ([hazelnut](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/586506/hazelnut-industry-expansion.pdf), [walnut](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/586590/walnut-industry-expansion.pdf), [chestnut](https://dpird-web.matrix.squiz.cloud/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/586487/chestnut-industry-expansion.pdf); government). A commercial nursery gives hazelnut as about 700-900 chill hours; hours and portions are not directly comparable ([Daleys](https://www.daleysfruit.com.au/nutintro.htm)).

**Hours bands (commercial).** High chill 650 hours or more, medium 450-650, low 150-450 (below about 7 C). Diggers says apples, apricots and European plums are high chill ([Daleys blog](https://blog.daleysfruit.com.au/2012/05/chill-hours-for-fruit-trees-calculation.html?m=1); [Diggers course](https://cms.diggers.com.au/courses/growing-food/m05/lesson-2-planning-your-fruit-growing)).

**Frost at flowering.**
- Hazelnut and walnut: late spring frosts are the key risk; the walnut sheet cites more than 100 potential frost days, the hazelnut sheet says regions with more than 100 average annual frost days are likely to get damaging late frosts. Orange is prone to late spring frost. Suitable regions named for walnut include the Central Tablelands (Orange), Adelaide Hills (Angaston) and parts of Tasmania; for hazelnut, the Adelaide Hills and Flinders and King Islands.
- Stone fruit: late frosts can destroy blossom; choose late-flowering varieties and avoid frost-prone gullies ([SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/how-to-grow-stone-fruit/4kk0te6sv)).
- Quince: highland districts get low temperatures that damage crops from before to after blossom; avoid valley floors and frost pockets. This is the only agency frost-at-flowering siting statement for pome or stone fruit in the sources reviewed, and it should not be extended to other species ([NSW DPI quince](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/agriculture/horticulture/pomes/other/quince-growing)).
- Avocado: mature trees tolerate about -4 C briefly, but not with flower buds, flowers or fruit ([Business Queensland](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/crops/fruit-veg/avocado/grow)).
- Citrus: young growth is frost-vulnerable ([Diggers](https://diggers.com.au/blogs/learn/citrus)).

## Biosecurity reminders

Only what the sources reviewed say. Rules change, so check your state agency before moving plants or fruit. Wider rules are on the [biosecurity and weeds page](biosecurity-and-weeds.md) and the [insect pests page](pests/insects.md).

> **Read first:** use any bait, spray or treatment exactly as its label or permit says. The sources reviewed give no bee-safety advice for the fruit fly baits mentioned below, so read the label.

**Fruit fly.**
- NSW: bait refuge trees and sheltered areas from late July to August, keep baiting through autumn and early winter, and note that empty winter traps do not mean the flies are gone. In winter Queensland fruit fly shelters in avocado, citrus, loquat, olive or avocado windbreaks and lemon trees near sheds ([NSW DPI Qfly](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/dpi/biosecurity/invasive-plants-and-animals/insect-pests/queensland-fruit-fly)).
- Queensland: pick ripe fruit before it falls; bag fallen fruit and leave it sealed in the sun for 3-7 days; pick bananas and avocados mature green; hosts include peach, orange, apple, pear, tomato and capsicum (practical, anecdotal, no trials; [SGA](https://www.sgaonline.org.au/queensland-fruit-fly/)).
- NT: harvest early and ripen indoors, weekly protein bait spray, remove fallen fruit and seal infested fruit in plastic in the sun, bag fruit. The two main NT pests are Bactrocera tryoni and B. jarvisi. Ti Tree and some surrounding properties are a fruit fly free area ([NT Agnote A20](https://daf.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/232914/425.pdf); [travelling within the NT](https://nt.gov.au/industry/agriculture/food-crops-plants-and-quarantine/plants-and-quarantine/travelling-within-the-nt)).
- WA: Mediterranean fruit fly is endemic and a declared pest. Soft stone fruit (apricot, peach, plum, nectarine) are the most attractive hosts and hard to keep fly-free; DPIRD suggests removing trees you cannot manage or harvest. Backyard control starts in spring and rests on sanitation, bait spot sprays, traps (at least two per tree), netting and neighbour coordination. Movement of citrus and summer fruit into the Ord River Irrigation Area is restricted from 1 April to 30 November each year ([DPIRD backyard management](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/sites/gateway/files/Medfly%20Backyard%20management.pdf); [DPIRD movement restrictions](https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/plant-biosecurity/movement-restrictions-plants-within-western-australia)).
- SA: permanent restrictions apply to bringing produce and plant material into SA and the Riverland Pest Free Area; outbreak zones are a 1.5 km red zone and a 15 km yellow zone for Queensland fruit fly (7.5 km for Mediterranean fruit fly) ([PIRSA fruit fly](https://fruitfly.sa.gov.au/); [outbreaks explained](https://fruitfly.sa.gov.au/outbreaks/outbreaks-explained)).

**Banana.**
- NT: interstate banana plants must be tissue-cultured, and uncertified north Queensland bananas are not allowed ([NT bananas](https://nt.gov.au/industry/agriculture/food-crops-plants-and-quarantine/fruit-crops/bananas)). An import permit is needed for banana and plantain plants ([Katherine Rural Review, 2019](https://industry.nt.gov.au/publications/primary-industry-publications/newsletters/regional-newsletters/archived-newsletters/krr/katherine-rural-review-092019/importing-plants-or-plant-products-into-the-northern-territory), a 2019 text; verify current requirements).
- Queensland: Panama TR4 is category 1 restricted matter and survives decades in soil; report suspect plants within 24 hours on 13 25 23 or 1800 084 881; backyard bananas would be affected; buy plants only from accredited suppliers and do not cut a suspect plant open. A biosecurity certificate is needed to move banana plants (not fruit), their soil or machinery out of any banana zone ([Business Queensland: identify TR4](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/crops/fruit-veg/bananas/panama-disease-tr4/identify); [movement within Qld](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/agriculture/biosecurity/plants/moving/restrictions/within-qld)).
- Banana freckle status and rules were not retrieved.

**Citrus.**
- NT: citrus and fortunella plants need a plant health certificate to enter the NT (2019 text; verify current requirements), and citrus canker is listed as a citrus pest in a Darwin City Council pamphlet ([Katherine Rural Review, 2019](https://industry.nt.gov.au/publications/primary-industry-publications/newsletters/regional-newsletters/archived-newsletters/krr/katherine-rural-review-092019/importing-plants-or-plant-products-into-the-northern-territory); [Darwin City Council orange](https://www.darwin.nt.gov.au/sites/default/files/old_7_publications_files/orange_0.pdf)).
- Citrus-specific movement rules and citrus canker status for the other states were not found (the NSW DPI citrus page failed to load).
- Citrus gall wasp: cut off infected branches and burn them; never compost ([Diggers](https://diggers.com.au/blogs/learn/citrus), commercial).
