# Native foods

> **How reliable is this page?** Only a minority of the native food plants gardeners ask about have a verified profile in our research notes. Profiles come from government, botanic garden and native plant society pages, but several rest on Wikipedia (muntries, kangaroo apple, bush tomato) and say so. Many plants are not covered at all (see [Gaps](#gaps)), and absence from this page is not a statement that a plant is safe or unsuitable. This page does not advise wild-harvesting. Buy plants or seed from Indigenous-led suppliers, and read [Safety first](#safety-first) before eating anything.

## Respect first

Bush foods carry Indigenous knowledge. How you source them matters as much as how you grow them.

- **Indigenous-led sourcing.** Indigenous Ecological Knowledge is part of Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP). The DPIRD WA guide lists the principles as respect, self-determination, consultation, free, prior and informed consent (FPIC), attribution and benefit sharing, and says protocols can be written into binding contracts. Kakadu plum (gubinge in WA) was commercialised using this knowledge without recognition of its source ([DPIRD WA, IEK guide](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/indigenous-ecological-knowledge-for-bushfood-businesses.pdf)).
- **Few Indigenous enterprises.** Australia has over 6,500 native plants but fewer than 15 are effectively developed for the bushfoods industry, and Aboriginal enterprises are estimated at under 15% of native food producers (DPIRD's own estimate) ([DPIRD WA](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/indigenous-ecological-knowledge-for-bushfood-businesses.pdf)). Buying from Indigenous-led growers is a direct way to help [inference].
- **Consent and culture.** Some plants are totemic or gender-specific. Consider whether it is culturally appropriate to grow a plant on a given Country, since some plants are tied to knowledge systems and may need permission. Native Title holders (PBCs/RNTBCs) must be consulted for harvest on Native Title land ([DPIRD WA, Setting up for success: bushfoods](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/setting-up-for-success---bushfoods.pdf)).
- **Seed collection.** Get permission from Traditional Owners well in advance (Native Title Representative Bodies usually refer you to Prescribed Body Corporates or ranger groups), pay Elders for guidance, protect location data and restricted men's and women's business knowledge, acknowledge contributors, and consult the AIATSIS Code of Ethics ([ANPC](https://www.anpc.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2.-Working-with-Indigenous-Australians.pdf)).
- **The laws, as the sources reviewed state them.** No Australian law specifically protects Indigenous knowledge. The NT Biological Resources Act 2006 requires benefit-sharing agreements and consent before collecting biological resources on Country, and the Queensland Biodiscovery Act 2004 supports benefit sharing. The status of WA's Biodiscovery Bill was unchecked in a 2021 guide. The Commonwealth EPBC Act's access and benefit-sharing obligations apply to commercial use of genetic resources, not non-commercial bushfood use ([DPIRD WA](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/indigenous-ecological-knowledge-for-bushfood-businesses.pdf)). Scientific collecting on Commonwealth land needs an EPBC Part 8A permit ([ANPC](https://www.anpc.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2.-Working-with-Indigenous-Australians.pdf)). Threatened and protected species need permits ([DPIRD WA bushfoods guide](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/setting-up-for-success---bushfoods.pdf)).
- **No wild harvest.** Harvesting in nature reserves and national parks is illegal ([Milkwood](https://www.milkwood.net/2014/05/21/foraging-warrigal-greens/), blog). In NSW, picking or possessing native plants is an offence under the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016; the park advice is to buy from markets or specialist sellers or grow at home ([NSW National Parks](https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/get-inspired/stories/bush-tucker-on-the-menu)). A commercial wild harvest of Kakadu plum on any NT tenure needs a take permit ([NT Government plan](https://environment.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/685676/kakadu-plum-tree-management-program-2019-2023.pdf)).
- **Credit the source.** When sharing recipes or selling, credit the Indigenous origin of the food and its uses [inference].

### Who the sources reviewed name

- **Worn Gundidj Nursery (Warrnambool, Vic): the one verified supplier.** Its own site describes it as Indigenous-led. It grows local-provenance native plants for revegetation, shelterbelts, agriculture and gardens, plus edible native seeds and bush foods. The page does not state its ownership structure ([Worn Gundidj](https://worngundidj.org.au/worn-gundidj-nursery/)). Local-provenance, regionally grown stock is likely to suit local conditions [inference].
- **Mayi Harvests (West Kimberley).** Founded in 2019 by Pat Mamanyjun Torres under Mamanyjun Tree Enterprises. The sources reviewed describe it as a Broome-based, Indigenous-led gubinge (Kakadu plum) harvester. Pickers hand-pick only fully ripe fruit, leave seeds, flowers and some fruit for wildlife, and take up to three pickings per tree. It employs up to 50 Aboriginal people per season, and a large share of proceeds goes to pickers. The figures are from a September 2020 article and may have changed ([The Fifth Estate](https://thefifthestate.com.au/business/indigenous-business-series/how-pat-torres-of-mayi-harvests-has-turned-the-kakadu-plum-into-a-social-benefit/)). The sources reviewed do not say it supplies home gardeners.
- **MAUDU Collective (Qld).** Described by Trade and Investment Queensland as Indigenous-led. It makes Indigi Fudge, was founded by Valerie Murray, uses Indigenous knowledge and aims to keep communities in the value chain. Two other enterprises in the same article (Figjam & Co, Helen Rose) are not stated to be Indigenous-led ([TIQ](https://www.tiq.qld.gov.au/news-and-events/news/from-ancient-knowledge-to-global-markets-queensland%E2%80%99s-native-botanicals-story)).
- **Palawa Kipli (Tasmania).** A Tasmanian Aboriginal Centre release (July 2026) describes it as "a 100% Aboriginal owned business". Its Native 9-Spice blend uses nine Tasmanian ingredients (kunzea, pepperberry, wattleseed, saltbush, kelp, sea salt, river mint, sea parsley and round-leaf mint bush). Profits go to the Tasmanian Aboriginal Centre, and it was made with a non-Aboriginal partner (Zea Gourmet) ([Tasmanian Aboriginal Centre](https://tacinc.com.au/2026/07/media-release-tasmanian-aboriginal-food-business-palawa-kipli-launches-first-ever-retail-product/)). This is a retail product, not a plant supplier.
- **Other examples in the DPIRD guide:** NAAKPA (founded 2018, eight Top End enterprises, Kakadu plum) and the Noongar Land Enterprise Group (six Noongar landholding groups, South West WA) ([DPIRD WA bushfoods guide](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/setting-up-for-success---bushfoods.pdf)).
- **Not verified:** IndigiGrow, Dalki Garringa Native Nursery, Marring Country nursery and IndigiEarth appeared in searches only. Tucker Bush is a bushfood nursery whose Indigenous status was not checked ([Tucker Bush](https://tuckerbush.com.au/warrigal-greens-tetragonia-tetragonioides)). Ownership, certification and plant health (for example myrtle rust status) of suppliers were not verified, and Indigenous-owned suppliers outside Victoria are not confirmed.
- **Northern Rivers, NSW (2017 article, may be dated):** the Bush Food Living Classroom at Federal was created with local Aboriginal custodians. Playing with Fire at Tintenbar is a native-foods farm, and the article does not say whether it is Aboriginal-owned ([Echo](https://www.echo.net.au/2017/08/now-native-foods/)).
- **Bush tomato** is marketed by Outback Pride and grown by named Aboriginal communities (Amata, Mimili, Dinahline, Nepabunna and Murray Bridge's Tangglun Piltengi Yunti), per a secondary source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solanum_centrale)).

## Safety first

> **Safety:** Do not eat any native plant on this page's authority alone. Identification and preparation are the risks. Every warning in our notes is listed here, and the same warnings sit next to each plant below.
>
> - **Identify first.** Some lookalikes are unsafe ([NSW National Parks](https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/get-inspired/stories/bush-tucker-on-the-menu)). The Tiwi Land Council says many species "are difficult to identify or may require special preparation to counteract toxicity", and advises non-Tiwi against eating these plants ([Tiwi Land Council](https://www.tiwilandcouncil.com/post/tiwi-food-plants)).
> - **Kangaroo apple (*Solanum aviculare*):** leaves and unripe fruit contain the toxic alkaloid solasodine. The source does not say whether ripe fruit is safe, so do not eat it on this page's authority ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solanum_aviculare)).
> - **Warrigal greens:** contain oxalates. Blanch (boiling water for up to 1 minute, drain, squeeze) and do not eat raw. No oxalate quantities are given. Picking at night is also said to reduce oxalate ([Milkwood](https://www.milkwood.net/2014/05/21/foraging-warrigal-greens/); [UTS Wara Burranura](https://waraburranura.uts.edu.au/plants/tetragonia-tetragonioides/)).
> - **Bush tomato (*Solanum centrale*):** the source gives no toxicity warning, but its photo caption refers to unripe fruit. Treat green or unripe fruit as unsafe unless a specific source says otherwise ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solanum_centrale)).
> - **Cycad seeds (Minta):** eaten raw they "may cause severe vomiting and sickness". The Tiwi method is roasting in ashes, cracking, dehusking, pounding and soaking the pulp for three days and nights ([Tiwi Land Council](https://www.tiwilandcouncil.com/post/tiwi-food-plants)). This is traditional knowledge, not a recipe [inference].
> - **Cheeky yam (*Dioscorea bulbifera*):** "toxic if not prepared correctly". Traditional detox is slicing, soaking the slices in running water for a day or more, then cooking. Another NT source says raw tubers and bulbils are very bitter and contain unnamed toxins ([Bininj Kunwok field guide mirror](https://pad.katalyst.com.au/plant_or_animals/cheeky-yam-round-yam); [Inspired NT](https://inspirednt.net/plants-collection/qfaffv3qsd001z9gv0w9e0yqlx5lau)). The Tiwi page says the tuber is dug when stems turn yellow and cooked overnight in coals.
> - **Bush pumpkin (Wupwarna):** the tuber is eaten only after extensive preparation, including overnight cooking ([Tiwi Land Council](https://www.tiwilandcouncil.com/post/tiwi-food-plants)).
> - **Long yam (*Dioscorea transversa*):** the NT sheet states no toxicity or detox, but absence of a warning is not proof of safety. The Tiwi page says it is eaten only after ceremony timing ([NT FF15](https://dpir.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/228008/ff15_bushtucker_in_top_end.pdf); [Tiwi Land Council](https://www.tiwilandcouncil.com/post/tiwi-food-plants)). Do not experiment with yams or cycads [inference].
> - **Wild grape (*Ampelocissus acetosa*):** "Seeds should not be eaten" ([NT FF15](https://dpir.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/228008/ff15_bushtucker_in_top_end.pdf)).
> - **Wild passionfruit (*Passiflora foetida*):** hairs on leaves and stems "can severely irritate skin" and, rubbed near the eyes, "can cause blindness". The skin of green fruit is "highly toxic". Too much ripe fruit is a laxative ([NT FF15](https://dpir.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/228008/ff15_bushtucker_in_top_end.pdf)).
> - **Red bush apple (*Syzygium suborbiculare*):** do not eat the seed ([Tiwi Land Council](https://www.tiwilandcouncil.com/post/tiwi-food-plants)).
> - **Water lily waterways:** saltwater crocodiles inhabit many of them ([NT FF15](https://dpir.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/228008/ff15_bushtucker_in_top_end.pdf)).
> - **Bunya cones:** male and female cones fall and can injure people. Keep an exclusion zone under fruiting trees ([ANPSA](https://anpsa.org.au/?p=6458)).
> - **Davidson plum:** the fruit is not recommended fresh. Use it stewed, in jam or in savoury dishes ([SGA](https://sgaonline.org.au/davidsons-plum)).
> - **Wattleseed:** roasting requirements and which Acacia species are toxic were not covered by the sources, so do not assume every wattle seed is edible ([Royal Society of Victoria](https://www.rsv.org.au/articles/wattleseed)).
> - **Murnong:** easily confused with dandelion or cat's ear ([Mallee Conservation](https://www.malleeconservation.com.au/blog/murnong)).
> - **Mountain pepper:** the ANBG page gives no toxicity or preparation warning, and absence of a warning is not proof of safety. Its berry-colour statement should not be relied on ([ANBG](https://www.anbg.gov.au/apu/plants/tasmlanc.html)).
> - **Old man saltbush:** leaves can hold up to 28% salt (a livestock issue). Use as a human food was not covered ([DPIRD WA](https://agric.wa.gov.au/sites/gateway/files/Saltdeck%20species%20-%20Atriplex%20nummularia%20-%20old%20man%20saltbush.pdf)).
> - **Quandong:** overwatering is fatal to the tree, and herbicides can pass from weeds to quandongs through shared roots ([Alice Springs News](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2020/10/19/the-queen-of-desert-fruit/); [PIRSA quandong fact sheet](https://www.ausbushfoods.com/bushfoodsonline/reports/Fact%20Sheets/pirsaquandong.pdf)).
> - **Anything not listed here** was not checked. That is not clearance.

- **Myrtle rust** infects Myrtaceae (over 300 hosts recorded), including lilly pilly. It starts as purple spots on soft new growth, then bright yellow pustules. Check plants before buying, do not move infected plants, wash clothing used in high-risk areas, and remove infected plants by bagging or solarising for 3-4 weeks (not in green waste bins) ([NSW DPI](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/biosecurity/plant/insect-pests-and-plant-diseases/myrtle-rust)). Myrtle rust is now in the NT ([NT Government](https://nt.gov.au/industry/agriculture/food-crops-plants-and-quarantine/nursery-and-garden-industry)). Riberry and lilly pilly are Myrtaceae, so treat them as susceptible [inference].
- **Named only, no profile:** the Echo article notes riberry, quandong, lilly pilly, midjinberry, mountain raspberry and pigface in the Northern Rivers, and says three of Australia's eight native raspberry species grow there ([Echo](https://www.echo.net.au/2017/08/now-native-foods/)). NSW National Parks mentions Dorrigo pepperberry (Dorrigo NP) and wattleseed from about 120 Acacia species across NSW ([NSW NP](https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/get-inspired/stories/bush-tucker-on-the-menu)). The Botanic Gardens list also names midgen berry, lilly pilly (*Syzygium smithii*) and pigface (*Carpobrotus glaucescens*) without detail ([Botanic Gardens](https://www.botanicgardens.org.au/discover-and-learn/gardening-home/growing-native-gardens-and-bush-foods/tuck-bush-tucker-garden)).
- **Macadamia** is named in the DPIRD guide as the only native species cultivated at large scale, but the sources reviewed have no home-growing profile ([DPIRD WA bushfoods guide](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/our-regions/setting-up-for-success-guides/setting-up-for-success---bushfoods.pdf)).
- **Shade-tolerant understorey** [inference]: warrigal greens, Davidson plum and lemon myrtle fit under tree canopy based on reported shade tolerance. They were not tested together in the sources.

Do not spray crowns | [PIRSA fact sheet](https://www.ausbushfoods.com/bushfoodsonline/reports/Fact%20Sheets/pirsaquandong.pdf); [Landscape SA](https://www.landscape.sa.gov.au/saal/news/whats-involved-in-managing-a-quandong-orchard); [SERCUL](https://www.sercul.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/BushTuckerFactSheet_Quandong.pdf) |
| **Old man saltbush** (*Atriplex nummularia*) | 175-500 mm rain. Drought-resistant, moderately salt tolerant. Poor in waterlogging (summer waterlogging can kill it) | Fodder and quandong host; used for dryland salinity management. Rarely produces volunteer seedlings, so not described as weedy | Leaves hold up to 28% salt (livestock). Use as human food not covered | [DPIRD WA](https://agric.wa.gov.au/sites/gateway/files/Saltdeck%20species%20-%20Atriplex%20nummularia%20-%20old%20man%20saltbush.pdf) |
| **Wattleseed** (*Acacia victoriae*, elegant wattle) | Wild in arid central Australia and some inland and coastal areas | Described as the most common edible wattle. Seed is ground to flour for damper; sealed pods keep seed viable up to 20 years on the ground | Roasting needs, toxic species, nitrogen fixation and weed risk outside native range were not covered | [RSV](https://www.rsv.org.au/articles/wattleseed); [AgriFutures](https://agrifutures.com.au/news/the-rise-of-ancient-native-bush-foods/) |

- **Quandong host.** In a glasshouse study, quandong with a host averaged 45 cm tall versus about 37 cm alone. *Myoporum parvifolium* and old man saltbush gave significantly greater quandong dry mass, and quandong did not harm the hosts ([CSIRO](https://publish.csiro.au/an/EA01093)). Other hosts reported: hop bush, narrow-leaf senna, ruby saltbush, red bud mallee, ghost gum and myoporum (Alice Springs); kikuyu, clovers, prostrate lucerne, acacias and melaleucas (PIRSA) ([Alice Springs News](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2020/10/19/the-queen-of-desert-fruit/); [PIRSA](https://www.ausbushfoods.com/bushfoodsonline/reports/Fact%20Sheets/pirsaquandong.pdf)). Quandong plus saltbush is a sensible low-input pairing [inference from two sources]; no field trial of the combination was found.
- **Quandong growing details.** Alice Springs grower account: sow 7-10 seeds near the host; fruit after 6-7 years; single grower. PIRSA: spacing 4-6 m between rows and 2-4 m within rows; about 80% seedling and 20% grafted; pollinator trees about 1:8 in clonal orchards; seed germination best around 18 C; a Quorn grower irrigates 24 L per tree weekly over winter and spring, kept away from the trunk. Grafting success ranged from 70% on a small group to 6 of 100 on a larger one. The quandong moth is the main pest; its control is under a permit, so ask PIRSA. Destroying fallen fruit reduces larvae. Maruku Arts has banned buying quandong timber because of pressure on populations ([Alice Springs News](https://alicespringsnews.com.au/2020/10/19/the-queen-of-desert-fruit/); [PIRSA](https://www.ausbushfoods.com/bushfoodsonline/reports/Fact%20Sheets/pirsaquandong.pdf); [Landscape SA](https://www.landscape.sa.gov.au/saal/news/whats-involved-in-managing-a-quandong-orchard)).
- **Sources disagree on timing.** PIRSA says quandong flowers in late autumn; the Noongar-language SERCUL sheet (name Dumbari) says flowers in summer and fruits in spring ([PIRSA](https://www.ausbushfoods.com/bushfoodsonline/reports/Fact%20Sheets/pirsaquandong.pdf); [SERCUL](https://www.sercul.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/BushTuckerFactSheet_Quandong.pdf)). Alice Springs says fruit ripens Aug-Sept. Planting months for tubestock were not sourced.
- **Wattles:** about 1,146 Acacia species exist and at least 150 were used by Indigenous Australians as food, medicine or materials. The sources' own claims of thousands of years of use differ and are unverified ([AgriFutures](https://agrifutures.com.au/news/the-rise-of-ancient-native-bush-foods/); [RSV](https://www.rsv.org.au/articles/wattleseed)). Wattles for seed should be the species local to your area [inference]. Cootamundra wattle invades bushland in wetter areas, and Sydney golden wattle is on a southern WA invasive garden list ([ANPSA](https://anpsa.org.au/APOL2008/jun08-2.html)).
- **Not verified for WA or SA:** bush tomato in gardens, sandalwood nut, native currant, native peach and karkalla.

## Native plantings that help food crops

- **Pollinators (strongest evidence).** Hort Innovation's PH16004 found that the amount and makeup of native vegetation affect crop pollinator numbers and diversity, with effects noticeable up to about 200 m into the crop. It recommends a wide range of local, easy-to-grow natives (most crop pollinators are generalists) as understorey, hedgerows or whole-area plantings, with flowers near the crop nearly year-round, including when the crop is not flowering ([Hort Innovation PH16004](https://www.horticulture.com.au/growers/help-your-business-grow/research-reports-publications-fact-sheets-and-more/ph16004)). The advice is limited to three South Australian cropping regions. Evidence: moderate.
- **Nesting habitat:** pithy-stemmed stick bundles (reed bees), open, compacted, well-drained soil (ground-nesting bees) and retained old paddock trees (hollows for feral honey bees and stingless bees) ([PH16004](https://www.horticulture.com.au/growers/help-your-business-grow/research-reports-publications-fact-sheets-and-more/ph16004)). Stingless bees pollinate macadamia, lychee, mango, avocado and watermelon, and blue-banded bees buzz-pollinate tomato ([BeeAware](https://beeaware.org.au/pollination/native-bees/native-bees-as-alternative-pollinators/)).
- **Insectary plantings.** AUSVEG recommends flowering native groundcovers, grasses and shrubs ideally within 50 m of crops, on non-cropping land such as headlands, shelterbelts, drainage lines and between rows, flowering as much of the year as possible. Control weeds, because they harbour pests more than natives do. Brown lacewings lay eggs in native grasses. The 2019 article reports no measured trial results ([AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/article/creating-a-permanent-habitat-for-beneficials-through-insectaries/)). Evidence: weak (advice, not measured).
- **No pest-control proof.** Biological pest control is listed in PH16004 only as a co-benefit needing further experimental evidence. No measured yield or pest-control outcomes from native insectaries were found, and no sourced lists of beneficial-insect plants, windbreak species or spacing ([PH16004](https://www.horticulture.com.au/growers/help-your-business-grow/research-reports-publications-fact-sheets-and-more/ph16004); [AUSVEG](https://ausveg.com.au/article/creating-a-permanent-habitat-for-beneficials-through-insectaries/)). For tested flowering strips beside vegetables, see [Principles](principles.md).
- **Other co-benefits listed:** livestock shade and shelter, erosion control and fire retardancy ([PH16004](https://www.horticulture.com.au/growers/help-your-business-grow/research-reports-publications-fact-sheets-and-more/ph16004)).
- **Edible natives as habitat [inference].** Kakadu plum has a sourced pollinator claim (flowers feed native bees and wasps), so it could double as habitat in its region. Saltbush is a plausible dryland shelter shrub, but no source tested it as a windbreak or beneficial-insect habitat. A Quorn grower suggested eremophila and small-leaf bluebush to attract wildlife ([Landscape SA](https://www.landscape.sa.gov.au/saal/news/whats-involved-in-managing-a-quandong-orchard)).
- **Weed risk.** Some natives become environmental weeds outside their natural range: Cootamundra wattle in wetter areas, sweet pittosporum in several states, and coastal tea tree and Sydney golden wattle on a southern WA invasive garden list ([ANPSA](https://anpsa.org.au/APOL2008/jun08-2.html)). Use locally native, local-provenance species.

## Gaps

No verified profile in our notes for these plants, so nothing is claimed about them. This is not a statement that they are safe or unsuitable.

- **Plants with no verified profile:** anise myrtle, riberry and other lilly pillies (cool-climate suitability and myrtle rust included), Illawarra plum, native raspberry (*Rubus parvifolius*), midyim or midgen berry, macadamia (conservation status and dog toxicity included), native rosella, bush banana, sea celery, karkalla or pigface (and hybridisation with the weed *Carpobrotus edulis*), native millet, native ginger (*Alpinia caerulea*), other yams, cluster fig, boab nuts, bullock bush, sandalwood nut, native currant, native peach, munyeroo, and Tasmanian specifics (native currants, apple berry, native cherry, kelp).
- **Partly covered:** Buchanania and long yam have only an NT sheet entry with no garden profile. Kakadu plum cultivation (fertiliser, irrigation, pests, cultivars) is early-stage, and an AgriFutures pollination project's findings were not read. Finger lime frost tolerance, water, thorn and pollination details were absent from the page read. Muntries ecological role is not covered. Pepperberry safety and preparation, Acacia seed processing and kangaroo apple ripe-fruit safety are unsourced.
- **Suppliers.** Only Worn Gundidj is verified. Indigenous-owned nurseries in WA, SA, NSW and Queensland are not verified; check Supply Nation, Traditional Owner groups and state landscape boards, and ask about local-provenance, disease-free stock.
- **Law.** WA's Biodiscovery Bill status was unchecked. No Kimberley Land Council page was reached.
- **Planting windows** for native tubestock were not provided by the sources. Native insectary species lists by region (tropical, Mediterranean, Tasmanian) are missing.
